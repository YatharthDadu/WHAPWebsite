Unit 9 Exam

Globalization

c. 1900 to present

Progress 0% · Score 0

Excerpt from an article by Tim Berners-Lee, inventor of the World Wide Web (1999):

"The Web is more a social creation than a technical one. I designed it for a social effect—to help people work together—and not as a technical toy. The ultimate goal of the Web is to support and improve our weblike existence in the world. We clump into families, associations, and companies. We develop trust across the miles and distrust around the corner."

Source: Tim Berners-Lee, Weaving the Web, 1999.

1. Based on the passage, the invention of the World Wide Web most directly facilitated which aspect of globalization?

Explanation: The Web was designed to connect people globally, overcoming geographic barriers to communication and collaboration.

2. The technological advancements in communication described in the passage have had the greatest impact on

Explanation: The internet and related technologies have fundamentally transformed how business, culture, and social interaction operate on a global scale.

Visual Description: A graph titled "Global Carbon Dioxide Emissions, 1900-2000" showing a sharp, exponential upward curve beginning around 1950, with the vast majority of emissions originating from the burning of fossil fuels (coal, oil, and natural gas).

Source: Based on historical climate and emissions data.

1. The sharp increase in carbon emissions shown in the graph after 1950 is most directly associated with

Explanation: The post-WWII period saw massive economic expansion, industrialization of developing nations, and increased consumerism, all powered heavily by fossil fuels.

2. Which of the following describes a major consequence of the trend shown in the graph?

Explanation: The environmental impact of fossil fuel use, specifically global warming, has become one of the most pressing and debated global issues.

Excerpt from a World Health Organization (WHO) report on the eradication of smallpox (1980):

"Having considered the development and results of the global program on smallpox eradication initiated by WHO in 1958... Declares solemnly that the world and all its peoples have won freedom from smallpox, which was a most devastating disease sweeping in epidemic form through many countries since earliest time."

Source: WHO Resolution, 1980.

1. The eradication of smallpox demonstrates the impact of

Explanation: The global vaccination campaign led by the WHO is a prime example of how international cooperation and medical advances can eliminate pandemic threats.

2. While vaccines eradicated diseases like smallpox, the late 20th century saw the emergence of new epidemic diseases primarily due to

Explanation: Increased travel, population density, and interaction with animal habitats have facilitated the rapid spread of new diseases like HIV/AIDS and COVID-19.

Excerpt from the agreement establishing the World Trade Organization (1994):

"Recognizing that their relations in the field of trade and economic endeavour should be conducted with a view to raising standards of living... expanding the production of and trade in goods and services... Being desirous of contributing to these objectives by entering into reciprocal and mutually advantageous arrangements directed to the substantial reduction of tariffs and other barriers to trade..."

Source: Marrakesh Agreement Establishing the World Trade Organization, 1994.

1. The primary goal of the World Trade Organization, as outlined in the passage, reflects the principles of

Explanation: The WTO promotes the reduction of trade barriers and tariffs to facilitate free trade and global economic integration.

2. A major result of the policies promoted by organizations like the WTO has been

Explanation: Free trade policies have allowed multinational corporations to move production to areas with cheaper labor, accelerating the growth of economies in places like China and Southeast Asia.

Excerpt from the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948):

"Article 1: All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights... Article 2: Everyone is entitled to all the rights and freedoms set forth in this Declaration, without distinction of any kind, such as race, colour, sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, property, birth or other status."

Source: United Nations, Universal Declaration of Human Rights, 1948.

1. The drafting of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights was most directly a response to

Explanation: The Holocaust and other atrocities prompted the international community to explicitly define and commit to protecting basic human rights on a global scale.

2. Movements that utilized the principles in this declaration to call for reform in the late 20th century included

Explanation: The language of universal, inherent rights provided a powerful framework for various marginalized groups to demand equality and justice globally.

Excerpt from a sociological study on global culture (2001):

"While walking down a street in Tokyo, one might see teenagers wearing American denim jeans, listening to British pop music on a Japanese-designed electronics device, and eating at an American fast-food chain, all while conversing in Japanese slang that incorporates English loan words."

Source: Generic sociological observation of globalization.

1. The scenario described in the passage is best understood as an example of

Explanation: The blending of international products and media into daily life illustrates how global trade and communication have created a shared global consumer culture.

2. A common critique of the cultural globalization described in the passage is that it

Explanation: Critics argue that "Americanization" or "McDonaldization" threatens to erode unique local traditions and languages in favor of a uniform global culture.

Excerpt from a speech by a leader of the Zapatista Army of National Liberation (EZLN) in Mexico (1994):

"The North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) is a death certificate for the Indigenous peoples of Mexico... We have nothing to lose, absolutely nothing, no decent roof over our heads, no land, no work, no poor health care, no food, no education... We are the product of 500 years of struggle."

Source: Subcomandante Marcos, Zapatista declaration, 1994.

1. The Zapatista uprising in 1994 was a direct reaction against

Explanation: The Zapatistas explicitly cited NAFTA as a threat to indigenous farmers who could not compete with heavily subsidized agricultural imports from the US.

2. The resistance described in the passage highlights which broader theme of the late 20th century?

Explanation: Anti-globalization movements often center on the argument that free trade policies disproportionately benefit wealthy corporations while harming local workers and the environment.

Excerpt from the mission statement of the International Monetary Fund (IMF):

"The IMF works to foster global monetary cooperation, secure financial stability, facilitate international trade, promote high employment and sustainable economic growth, and reduce poverty around the world."

Source: IMF official website.

1. Institutions like the IMF and the World Bank were primarily established to

Explanation: Created at the Bretton Woods Conference in 1944, these institutions were designed to rebuild war-torn economies and prevent the economic instability that contributed to the Great Depression and WWII.

2. A frequent criticism of institutions like the IMF by developing nations is that

Explanation: Critics argue that IMF conditions often force developing nations to privatize resources and cut welfare programs, causing hardship for their citizens.

Excerpt from a United Nations report on global inequality (2020):

"While the past century has seen unprecedented advancements in technology, medicine, and overall global wealth, the benefits have not been shared equally. Billions have been lifted out of extreme poverty, yet the gap between the wealthiest individuals and nations and the poorest continues to widen, exacerbated by challenges like climate change and pandemics."

Source: Synthesized UN report themes.

1. Based on the passage, the most significant change in the globalized world has been

Explanation: The passage acknowledges that globalization has driven massive technological progress and wealth creation, lifting many out of poverty.

2. Which aspect of the passage represents a major historical continuity despite the changes brought by globalization?

Explanation: Just as in the eras of colonialism and early industrialization, the modern globalized economy continues to struggle with the unequal distribution of wealth and resources.
