Excerpt from Jean-Jacques Rousseau, The Social Contract, 1762:

"Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains... What then is the government? An intermediate body set up between the subjects and the Sovereign, to secure their mutual correspondence, charged with the execution of the laws and the maintenance of liberty, both civil and political."

Source: Jean-Jacques Rousseau, The Social Contract, 1762

1. The ideas expressed in the excerpt most directly challenged which of the following political concepts?

Explanation: Rousseau's assertion that government is an intermediate body set up to maintain liberty challenges the concept of the divine right of kings, which posits that monarchs derive their authority directly from God, not the people.

2. Rousseau's philosophy as seen in the excerpt most directly influenced which of the following events?

Explanation: Rousseau's ideas about the social contract and popular sovereignty were foundational to the ideological justifications for the French Revolution.

3. Which of the following movements also heavily relied on the Enlightenment principles expressed in the excerpt?

Explanation: The abolitionist movement drew heavily on Enlightenment ideals of natural rights and human liberty, similar to those expressed by Rousseau.

4. The excerpt best illustrates which of the following broad historical developments of the 18th century?

Explanation: The Enlightenment involved applying human reason to understand and improve society and government, as demonstrated by Rousseau's analysis of the social contract.

Excerpt from Simon Bolivar, The Jamaica Letter, 1815:

"We have been harassed by a conduct which has not only deprived us of our rights but has kept us in a sort of permanent infancy with regard to public affairs. If we could at least have managed our domestic affairs and our internal administration, we could have acquainted ourselves with the processes and mechanics of public affairs."

Source: Simon Bolivar, The Jamaica Letter, 1815

5. Bolivar's grievances in the excerpt were most directly rooted in the policies of which of the following empires?

Explanation: Bolivar was a leader of the independence movements in South America against the Spanish Empire.

6. The political ideas expressed in the excerpt most clearly reflect the influence of which of the following?

Explanation: Bolivar's complaints about being deprived of rights and kept in political infancy reflect Enlightenment ideas about liberty, self-government, and natural rights.

7. Which of the following groups in Latin American society would most likely have shared Bolivar's sentiments in the excerpt?

Explanation: Creoles (people of European descent born in the Americas) were the primary group leading the independence movements, as they felt marginalized by Spanish imperial policies that favored Peninsulares.

8. The struggle described in the excerpt ultimately led to which of the following outcomes in Latin America?

Explanation: Despite Bolivar's hopes for unity, the Latin American independence movements resulted in the formation of several distinct independent nation-states.

Excerpt from Adam Smith, The Wealth of Nations, 1776:

"It is not from the benevolence of the butcher, the brewer, or the baker, that we expect our dinner, but from their regard to their own interest. We address ourselves, not to their humanity but to their self-love, and never talk to them of our own necessities but of their advantages."

Source: Adam Smith, The Wealth of Nations, 1776

9. Adam Smith's argument in the excerpt is best understood as a defense of which of the following economic systems?

Explanation: Smith is describing the foundational principle of free-market capitalism, where individuals pursuing their own self-interest unintentionally benefit society as a whole.

10. The ideas expressed by Smith were most directly a reaction against which of the following economic practices?

Explanation: Smith's laissez-faire ideas were largely a critique of mercantilist policies, which involved heavy government regulation of trade and the economy.

11. Which of the following developments in the 19th century most strongly contradicted the economic principles advocated by Smith in the excerpt?

Explanation: The rise of labor unions and government intervention in the economy (such as labor laws) contradicted Smith's ideal of a completely unregulated free market.

12. Smith's concept of self-interest leading to economic prosperity was most readily adopted by which of the following groups during the Industrial Revolution?

Explanation: Industrial capitalists embraced Smith's theories because they provided an ideological justification for their pursuit of profit without government interference.

Excerpt from Visual Description:

"A colorful Japanese woodblock print from the 1870s showing the interior of the Tomioka Silk Mill. Dozens of Japanese women in traditional dress are working at modern, Western-style steam-powered reeling machines. A European supervisor in a suit stands in the background."

Source: Ukiyo-e woodblock print, Meiji Era Japan, c. 1870s

13. The image best illustrates which of the following policies of the Meiji Restoration?

Explanation: The Tomioka Silk Mill was a model factory established by the Meiji government to introduce modern, Western industrial techniques to Japan.

14. The presence of female workers in the mill reflects which of the following global trends of the Industrial Revolution?

Explanation: Similar to early industrialization in Britain and the US, Japan relied heavily on young female labor in its initial textile factories.

15. The European supervisor in the background signifies which of the following aspects of Japanese industrialization?

Explanation: The Meiji government frequently hired foreign experts (oyatoi gaikokujin) to teach modern industrial and technological skills to Japanese workers and managers.

16. Which of the following was a primary motivation for the industrialization efforts depicted in the image?

Explanation: The slogan "Enrich the country, strengthen the military" (Fukoku Kyohei) drove Meiji industrialization, aiming to prevent Japan from being subjugated by Western powers like China had been.

Excerpt from an article in The Times of London, August 1858, regarding the first transatlantic telegraph cable:

"The Atlantic is dried up, and we become in reality as well as in wish one country... The telegraph has annihilated both space and time in the transmission of intelligence."

Source: The Times, London, August 1858

17. The technological development described in the excerpt most directly contributed to which of the following?

Explanation: The telegraph allowed for rapid communication across vast distances, enabling the coordination of trade, finance, and markets on a global scale.

18. Which of the following was another major communication or transportation technology that had a similar impact to the telegraph in the 19th century?

Explanation: Like the telegraph, steam-powered railways (and steamships) dramatically reduced the time and cost of communication and transportation, "annihilating space and time."

19. The sentiments expressed in the excerpt best reflect which of the following worldviews prevalent in 19th-century Europe?

Explanation: The quote reflects a highly optimistic, progressive view of technology's ability to overcome natural barriers and unite the world.

Excerpt from Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, The Communist Manifesto, 1848:

"The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles... The modern bourgeois society that has sprouted from the ruins of feudal society has not done away with class antagonisms. It has but established new classes, new conditions of oppression, new forms of struggle in place of the old ones. Our epoch, the epoch of the bourgeoisie, possesses, however, this distinct feature: it has simplified class antagonisms. Society as a whole is more and more splitting up into two great hostile camps, into two great classes directly facing each other — Bourgeoisie and Proletariat."

Source: Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, The Communist Manifesto, 1848

20. Marx and Engels' analysis in the excerpt is a direct response to which of the following historical developments?

Explanation: The Communist Manifesto was written as a critique of the capitalist system and the harsh social conditions created by the Industrial Revolution.

21. According to the excerpt, what is the primary driving force of history?

Explanation: The first sentence explicitly states that "The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles."

22. Which of the following groups represents the "Proletariat" mentioned in the excerpt?

Explanation: In Marxist terminology, the proletariat is the class of wage-earners in a capitalist society whose only significant material value is their labor-power, primarily the industrial working class.

23. The ideas presented in the excerpt most directly inspired which of the following later historical events?

Explanation: Marxist ideology was the foundational philosophy of the Bolsheviks who led the Russian Revolution and established the Soviet Union.

Excerpt from the testimony of Matthew Crabtree to the Sadler Committee, British Parliament, 1832:

"What age are you? — Twenty-two.

What is your occupation? — A blanket manufacturer.

Have you ever been employed in a factory? — Yes.

At what age did you first go to work in one? — Eight.

How long did you continue in that occupation? — Four years.

Will you state the hours of labour at the period when you first went to the factory, in ordinary times? — From 6 in the morning to 8 at night.

With what intervals for refreshment and rest? — An hour at noon.

When trade was brisk what were your hours? — From 5 in the morning to 9 in the evening."

Source: Report of the Select Committee on Factory Children's Labour (Sadler Report), 1832

24. The testimony in the excerpt provides evidence for which of the following social effects of early industrialization?

Explanation: The testimony details a child starting work at age eight and working up to 16 hours a day, highlighting the harsh realities of child labor.

25. Investigations like the one depicted in the excerpt eventually led to which of the following outcomes in industrialized nations?

Explanation: Reports like the Sadler Report exposed abuses and helped spur the passage of reform laws, such as the Factory Acts in Britain, which began to regulate child labor.

26. The grueling hours described in the excerpt were largely made possible by which of the following technological innovations?

Explanation: The introduction of gas lighting in factories allowed them to operate long after sunset, extending the working day significantly.

27. The social conditions described in the excerpt contributed most directly to the rise of which of the following ideologies?

Explanation: The harsh conditions of the working class, including child labor, provided fertile ground for socialist thinkers who argued for a more equitable distribution of wealth and better conditions.

Excerpt from Josiah Strong, Our Country: Its Possible Future and Its Present Crisis, 1885:

"It seems to me that God, with infinite wisdom and skill, is training the Anglo-Saxon race for an hour sure to come in the world's future... Then this race of unequalled energy, with all the majesty of numbers and the might of wealth behind it—the representative, let us hope, of the largest liberty, the purest Christianity, the highest civilization—having developed peculiarly aggressive traits calculated to impress its institutions upon mankind, will spread itself over the earth."

Source: Josiah Strong, Our Country, 1885

28. The ideas expressed in the excerpt are best understood as an example of which of the following justifications for imperialism?

Explanation: Strong's belief that the Anglo-Saxon race is divinely appointed to spread its "highest civilization" and "purest Christianity" is a classic articulation of the civilizing mission rationale for imperialism.

29. Which of the following scientific theories was often misapplied in the late 19th century to support the racial views expressed in the excerpt?

Explanation: Social Darwinism applied concepts of "survival of the fittest" to human societies, arguing that technologically advanced races were naturally superior and destined to dominate others.

30. The ideology in the excerpt most directly supported the expansion of which of the following empires in the late 19th century?

Explanation: Josiah Strong was an American clergyman, and his writings were widely used to justify US imperial expansion in places like the Philippines, Hawaii, and Puerto Rico.

31. Which of the following would be the most likely response of colonized peoples to the ideology expressed in the excerpt?

Explanation: Colonized peoples generally resented and resisted the racist justifications of imperialism, leading to the development of nationalist movements seeking self-determination.

Excerpt from Visual Description:

"A political map of Africa from 1914, showing the continent almost entirely divided into colonies possessed by European powers such as Britain, France, Germany, Belgium, Italy, and Portugal. Only Ethiopia and Liberia are shown as independent."

Source: Historical Atlas Map, Africa 1914

32. The political boundaries shown on the map were largely formalized at which of the following events?

Explanation: The Berlin Conference established the rules for the European partition of Africa, leading to the rapid colonization known as the "Scramble for Africa."

33. Which of the following technological advantages best explains how European powers were able to establish the colonial control shown on the map?

Explanation: The Maxim gun, quinine (to prevent malaria), and steam-powered riverboats allowed Europeans to penetrate the African interior and overcome indigenous resistance.

34. The borders drawn on the map by European powers had which of the following long-term consequences for African societies?

Explanation: European borders were drawn arbitrarily based on European interests, ignoring indigenous realities, which has caused enduring political instability in post-colonial Africa.

35. The independence of Ethiopia, as shown on the map, was primarily due to which of the following?

Explanation: Under Emperor Menelik II, Ethiopia successfully modernized its military and defeated the Italian army at Adwa in 1896, preserving its independence.

Excerpt from Lin Zexu's Letter to Queen Victoria, 1839:

"Suppose there were people from another country who carried opium for sale to England and seduced your people into buying and smoking it; certainly your honorable ruler would deeply hate it and be bitterly aroused... May you, O King, check your wicked and sift your vicious people before they come to China, in order to guarantee the peace of your nation."

Source: Lin Zexu, Letter to Queen Victoria, 1839

36. Lin Zexu's letter was a direct response to which of the following economic practices by the British?

Explanation: The British East India Company grew opium in India and smuggled it into China to pay for Chinese goods (tea, silk, porcelain) because China demanded silver, creating a trade imbalance for Britain.

37. The failure of Lin Zexu's diplomatic efforts to stop the drug trade resulted most immediately in which of the following?

Explanation: When Lin Zexu confiscated and destroyed British opium, the British government responded with military force, sparking the First Opium War (1839-1842).

38. Which of the following was a major consequence for China following the conflict caused by the issues described in the excerpt?

Explanation: The Treaty of Nanjing and subsequent treaties forced China to open ports, cede Hong Kong, and grant special privileges to foreigners, severely compromising its sovereignty.

39. The tone and content of the excerpt best reflect which of the following traditional Chinese attitudes toward foreign nations?

Explanation: Lin Zexu's tone is somewhat condescending, assuming the moral high ground and appealing to the Queen to control her "vicious people," reflecting the traditional Sinocentric worldview.

Excerpt from a British consular report on the Chincha Islands of Peru, 1874:

"The guano is dug out by Chinese coolies... They are kept at work by the whip... The stench is intolerable... The quantity exported last year was about 350,000 tons, the greater part of which went to Great Britain."

Source: British Consular Report, 1874

40. The export of guano described in the excerpt was primarily used for which of the following purposes in industrializing nations?

Explanation: Guano (bird droppings) is highly rich in nitrogen and was highly sought after as a fertilizer to boost agricultural yields in Europe and North America to feed expanding urban populations.

41. The use of "Chinese coolies" mentioned in the excerpt reflects which of the following global labor trends of the 19th century?

Explanation: As slavery was abolished, empires and nations turned to indentured servitude, often using labor from Asia (like Chinese and Indian "coolies"), to perform harsh labor in plantations and mines.

42. The economic relationship described in the excerpt, where Peru exports a raw material primarily to Great Britain, is characteristic of which of the following?

Explanation: Latin American countries often developed export economies focused on single raw materials (like guano, copper, or beef) to supply the industrialized centers, leaving them economically vulnerable.

43. Which of the following was a negative consequence for countries like Peru that relied heavily on commodity exports like guano?

Explanation: Economies based on a single export commodity were highly vulnerable to price fluctuations in the global market or the invention of synthetic alternatives (like artificial fertilizers later on).

Excerpt from Visual Description:

"A British political cartoon from the 1850s showing a giant, heavily armed British soldier standing astride the Indian subcontinent. In his hands, he holds bags of money and cotton. Below him, smaller figures of Indian princes are bowing, while Indian peasants are working in fields."

Source: 19th-century political cartoon

44. The cartoon best illustrates which of the following concepts?

Explanation: The giant soldier holding wealth extracted from India (money and cotton) while natives bow or work clearly depicts the exploitative nature of British economic imperialism.

45. The cotton held by the soldier was primarily destined for which of the following locations?

Explanation: India was forced to supply raw cotton to feed the booming textile industry in Britain (especially after the US Civil War disrupted American cotton supplies).

46. Which of the following was a direct consequence of the economic relationship depicted in the cartoon for the Indian economy?

Explanation: British policies intentionally stifled India's traditional, highly skilled textile manufacturing to force India to become a market for cheaper, mass-produced British textiles and a supplier of raw materials.

47. The presence of the heavily armed soldier in the cartoon most directly references which of the following historical realities?

Explanation: The British used military force, initially through the East India Company's army (which included sepoys), to conquer and maintain control over India's economy and resources.

Excerpt from a letter by Margaret McCarthy, an Irish immigrant in New York, to her family in Ireland, 1850:

"Come you all together courageously and bid adieu to that lovely place, the land of our birth... for you may be sure the potatoes will fail again... Here is a great many from our parish and they are all doing well... I have $2 a week and my board... I am not sorry for coming."

Source: Margaret McCarthy, Immigrant Letter, 1850

48. The primary "push factor" prompting the migration described in the excerpt was which of the following?

Explanation: The letter references the failure of the potatoes, which refers to the Irish Potato Famine (1845-1852), a catastrophic ecological disaster that caused massive starvation and emigration.

49. The wages and conditions described by the author ("$2 a week and my board") represent which of the following "pull factors"?

Explanation: For impoverished immigrants escaping famine, the prospect of steady wage labor (often in domestic service or factories) in the rapidly growing US economy was a strong pull factor.

50. The fact that "a great many from our parish" are already in New York illustrates which of the following patterns of migration?

Explanation: Chain migration occurs when migrants follow previous migrants from their home community to a specific destination, often relying on their networks for housing and jobs.
