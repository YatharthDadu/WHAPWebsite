c. 1450 to c. 1750

Excerpt from an account by a Venetian merchant in Constantinople, c. 1453:

"The Sultan Mehmed II entered the city and immediately ordered the great church of Hagia Sophia to be cleared of its Christian relics and transformed into a mosque. His armies, utilizing massive bronze cannons, had breached the ancient walls that had stood for a thousand years. With this victory, the Ottomans have secured their bridge between Asia and Europe, cementing their control over the vital trade routes."

Source: Fictionalized composite of Venetian merchant accounts of the fall of Constantinople.

1. Which of the following technological developments most directly enabled the Ottoman success described in the passage?

Explanation: The passage explicitly mentions "massive bronze cannons" which represent the critical gunpowder technology that allowed land-based empires like the Ottomans to expand.

2. The transformation of the Hagia Sophia into a mosque best illustrates which of the following historical processes of the period 1450-1750?

Explanation: Conquering rulers often repurposed or built monumental architecture, like converting Hagia Sophia, to legitimize their power and demonstrate the triumph of their faith.

3. The consolidation of Ottoman control over the "vital trade routes" mentioned in the passage most directly led to which of the following?

Explanation: Ottoman control of eastern Mediterranean and overland routes incentivized Western European states to fund maritime exploration to find direct sea routes to Asian markets.

4. The expansion of the Ottoman Empire described in the passage contributed most directly to which of the following conflicts?

Explanation: The expansion of the Ottoman Empire led to border and religious (Sunni-Shi'a) conflicts with the neighboring Safavid Empire in the Middle East.

Excerpt from the Laws of Military Houses (Buke Shohatto) issued by the Tokugawa Shogunate, 1635:

"The daimyo [feudal lords] are required to alternate their residence every other year in Edo. Their wives and children must remain in Edo at all times as hostages to the Shogun. Marriages among the daimyo cannot be contracted without the explicit permission of the Shogun. All repairs to castles must be reported, and new castles are strictly forbidden."

Source: Tokugawa Shogunate, Edicts of 1635.

5. The policy of alternate attendance described in the passage was primarily designed to accomplish which of the following?

Explanation: The Tokugawa Shogunate used the alternate attendance system (sankin kotai) to keep the daimyo financially drained and under close surveillance, thereby centralizing state power.

6. The requirement for daimyo to leave their families as "hostages" in Edo is most similar to which other historical method of imperial control?

Explanation: Both Louis XIV and the Tokugawa shoguns required powerful nobles to reside at the capital under the ruler's eye to prevent rebellion and centralize authority.

7. Which of the following was a major unintended economic consequence of the policy described in the passage?

Explanation: The constant travel of daimyo and their retinues to and from Edo stimulated the development of roads, roadside towns, and a thriving merchant class to cater to their needs.

8. The restrictions on castle building and marriages in the edict reflect which broader trend of the period 1450-1750?

Explanation: Throughout this period, successful land-based empires centralized power by systematically undermining the military and political autonomy of traditional feudal or landholding elites.

Excerpt from a letter by the Mughal Emperor Akbar to the Spanish King Philip II, 1582:

"It has been brought to our notice that in many regions, people belonging to various faiths suffer great persecution at the hands of those who hold different beliefs. Realizing that there is truth in all religions, we have decided to associate with the learned men of all faiths—Hindus, Muslims, Christians, and Zoroastrians. We seek to establish a harmonious society where no one is harassed for their religion."

Source: Translated correspondence from the Mughal Court.

9. Akbar's policies as described in the passage most directly contrast with the policies of which of the following contemporary empires?

Explanation: While Akbar promoted religious tolerance and syncretism, Philip II's Spain vigorously persecuted religious minorities through the Inquisition.

10. The establishment of "a harmonious society" mentioned by Akbar was practically implemented through which of the following state actions?

Explanation: Akbar famously abolished the jizya, a tax traditionally levied on non-Muslim subjects, as a key part of his policy of religious tolerance.

11. Akbar's interactions with "learned men of all faiths" eventually led him to develop which of the following?

Explanation: Akbar's religious inquiries culminated in his creation of the Din-i Ilahi, which blended elements of Islam, Hinduism, Christianity, and Zoroastrianism.

12. Despite Akbar's efforts, how did religious policies in the Mughal Empire change under later rulers like Aurangzeb?

Explanation: Later Mughal emperors, particularly Aurangzeb, reversed Akbar's tolerant policies, reinstated the jizya, and alienated Hindu subjects, leading to internal conflicts.

Excerpt from a Portuguese navigator's manual, c. 1490:

"To sail safely along the coast of Africa and out into the Great Ocean, the mariner must not rely solely on the sight of land. He must master the astrolabe to determine his latitude by the sun and stars. Furthermore, our newly designed ships, rigged with triangular lateen sails, allow us to tack against the contrary winds of the South Atlantic, a feat impossible with the old square-rigged vessels."

Source: Composite text based on late 15th-century Portuguese navigation treatises.

13. The technologies mentioned in the passage were most directly adapted by Europeans from which of the following regions?

Explanation: Technologies like the astrolabe, lateen sail, and magnetic compass were developed in the Islamic world and Asia (China) before being adopted and improved by Europeans.

14. The "newly designed ships" mentioned in the passage most likely refer to which of the following vessels?

Explanation: The caravel was a highly maneuverable ship developed by the Portuguese in the 15th century, notable for its use of lateen sails to sail against the wind.

15. The mastery of the maritime techniques described in the passage most directly resulted in which of the following?

Explanation: These navigational advancements allowed the Portuguese to round the Cape of Good Hope and establish fortified trading posts across the Indian Ocean basin.

16. Which of the following states was the primary early sponsor of the innovations and voyages described in the excerpt?

Explanation: Prince Henry the Navigator of Portugal sponsored much of the early research, mapmaking, and expeditions that advanced European maritime technology.

Excerpt from an agricultural treatise by a European botanist, c. 1610:

"This wondrous root, brought from the mountains of the New World, grows abundantly even in poor, rocky soil where wheat will fail. It requires less labor and yields a harvest capable of feeding a family for a year on a mere fraction of land. Though initially viewed with suspicion and thought only fit for livestock, it is slowly becoming the staple food of the peasantry across the continent."

Source: Fictionalized composite of early European writings on the potato.

17. The crop described in the passage is most likely which of the following?

Explanation: The potato, native to the Andes in the Americas, was introduced to Europe where it could grow in poor soil and yielded high caloric output, becoming a peasant staple.

18. The introduction of the crop described in the passage most directly contributed to which of the following in Afro-Eurasia?

Explanation: New World crops like potatoes and maize provided higher caloric yields per acre, leading to significant population growth across Europe and Asia.

19. While the Americas contributed the crop described above to Afro-Eurasia, which of the following was a major biological transfer from Afro-Eurasia to the Americas that had devastating demographic consequences?

Explanation: The introduction of Afro-Eurasian diseases to the Americas resulted in the Great Dying, wiping out up to 90% of the indigenous population who lacked immunity.

20. The agricultural exchange described in the passage is best understood in the context of which broader historical process?

Explanation: The Columbian Exchange refers to the unprecedented transfer of plants, animals, diseases, and people between the Americas and the Old World following 1492.

Excerpt from the royal decree establishing the encomienda system by the Spanish Crown, c. 1512:

"To ensure the pacification of the newly discovered islands and the instruction of the native Indians in the Holy Catholic Faith, we grant our loyal subjects the right to extract labor and tribute from the Indians residing on the lands assigned to them. In return, the Spanish lords must protect these Indians, treat them justly, and ensure their religious conversion."

Source: Adapted from the Laws of Burgos, 1512.

21. The encomienda system described in the passage most closely resembles which of the following labor systems?

Explanation: The encomienda system was modeled on European feudalism, where a lord was granted rights to the labor and tribute of peasants tied to a specific territory.

22. Despite the royal decree's mandate to "treat them justly," the primary result of the encomienda system for indigenous populations was:

Explanation: In practice, encomenderos brutally exploited indigenous labor in mines and on plantations, contributing significantly to the demographic collapse of native populations.

23. Which of the following individuals became a prominent critic of the abuses of the system described in the passage?

Explanation: Bartolomé de las Casas, a Spanish Dominican friar, was a fierce critic of the encomienda system and the brutal treatment of indigenous peoples, writing extensively on the subject.

24. As the indigenous population declined precipitously, how did the Spanish and other European empires primarily adapt their labor systems in the Americas?

Explanation: To replace the dying indigenous workforce, European empires initiated the trans-Atlantic slave trade to bring enslaved African labor to work on plantations and in mines.

Excerpt from "England's Treasure by Forraign Trade" by Thomas Mun, 1664:

"The ordinary means therefore to increase our wealth and treasure is by Foreign Trade, wherein we must ever observe this rule; to sell more to strangers yearly than we consume of theirs in value. For that part of our stock which is not returned to us in wares must necessarily be brought home in treasure [gold and silver]. We must strive to export our manufactured goods and import raw materials from our colonies."

Source: Thomas Mun, English economist and director of the East India Company.

25. The economic philosophy described in the passage is best identified as which of the following?

Explanation: The passage outlines mercantilism, an economic theory emphasizing a positive balance of trade, accumulation of bullion (gold/silver), and the extraction of raw materials from colonies.

26. According to the passage, what role were colonies expected to play in this economic system?

Explanation: Under mercantilism, colonies were strictly regulated to supply cheap raw materials to the mother country and purchase its more expensive manufactured goods.

27. The desire to "bring home treasure" in the form of gold and silver most directly fueled which of the following global networks?

Explanation: European desire to accumulate bullion led to the massive extraction of silver from Spanish colonies (like Potosí), which was then used to purchase Asian goods, creating the first truly global currency network.

28. Which of the following types of organizations was primarily used by the English and Dutch to implement the policies described in the passage?

Explanation: Joint-stock companies, like the British and Dutch East India Companies, were granted monopolies by their governments to conduct trade, build outposts, and implement mercantilist policies globally.

Excerpt from an account of the Pueblo Revolt, 1680:

"The Indian leader Popé orchestrated a unified rebellion across dozens of pueblos. The natives, tired of the heavy tribute, the forced labor in the encomiendas, and the suppression of their ancestral religious practices by the Franciscan friars, rose up in unison. They burned the churches, killed the priests, and drove the Spanish entirely out of the province of New Mexico, reclaiming their land for over a decade."

Source: Fictionalized composite of Spanish historical accounts of the 1680 revolt.

29. The Pueblo Revolt is best understood as an example of which of the following historical processes?

Explanation: The Pueblo Revolt was a coordinated indigenous uprising against the political, economic (encomienda), and religious oppression of the Spanish colonial regime.

30. The destruction of churches and targeting of priests highlights which specific grievance of the Pueblo people?

Explanation: The Spanish aggressively suppressed indigenous religions and forced conversions; the targeting of churches shows the revolt was largely a rejection of this religious oppression.

31. How does the Pueblo Revolt compare to King Philip's War (Metacom's War) in New England during the same era?

Explanation: Like the Pueblo Revolt, Metacom's War was a significant, unified armed resistance by indigenous peoples against the expansion and policies of European (British) settlers.

32. What was a long-term consequence of the Pueblo Revolt after the Spanish eventually reconquered the territory?

Explanation: After the reconquest, the Spanish were more cautious, abolished the encomienda system in the region, and tolerated some syncretic religious practices to avoid another massive uprising.

Description of an 18th-century Mexican Casta Painting:

The painting features sixteen distinct panels. Each panel displays a father, a mother, and their child, meticulously illustrating the racial mixing in the Spanish Americas. The first panel shows a Spanish man (Español) and an Indigenous woman (India) with their Mestizo child. Subsequent panels show combinations such as a Spaniard and a woman of African descent producing a Mulatto child, and further complex generational mixtures, with lower panels depicting poorer clothing and lower social status.

Source: Description of a typical 18th-century Casta painting from New Spain.

33. The social hierarchy depicted in the casta paintings was primarily based on which of the following?

Explanation: The casta system was a complex racial hierarchy created by the Spanish elites in the Americas to maintain power, basing social status strictly on racial background and European bloodlines (limpieza de sangre).

34. The creation of the casta system was a direct result of which of the following demographic realities in the Spanish colonies?

Explanation: Because early European migration consisted mostly of single men, significant racial mixing (mestizaje) occurred, prompting colonial authorities to create a system to categorize the mixed-race population.

35. In the hierarchy depicted, which group held the highest political and economic power, often serving as viceroys and high church officials?

Explanation: Peninsulares held the highest status and monopolized the top administrative and religious posts, creating tension with the Creoles, who were wealthy but excluded from the highest offices.

36. The social structure illustrated in the casta paintings contrasts most sharply with the social structure of which other colonial region?

Explanation: British North American colonies typically saw migration of family units, leading to less racial mixing and the development of a stricter binary racial system (white/black) compared to the nuanced casta system.

Excerpt from the Sacred Edict of the Kangxi Emperor, Qing Dynasty, 1670:

"Highly esteem filial piety and brotherly submission, in order to give due weight to social relations. Magnify family and clan, in order to promote peace and harmony. Cultivate peace and concord in your neighborhoods, in order to prevent quarrels and litigations. Give importance to agriculture and sericulture [silk production], in order to ensure a sufficiency of clothing and food."

Source: The Sacred Edict of the Kangxi Emperor, 1670.

37. The values promoted in the Kangxi Emperor's decree are most deeply rooted in which of the following philosophical traditions?

Explanation: The emphasis on filial piety, social relations, family harmony, and agriculture are foundational elements of Confucian philosophy, which the Qing rulers utilized to legitimize their rule.

38. Why might the Kangxi Emperor, a Manchu ruler of the Qing Dynasty, find it politically necessary to issue decrees emphasizing the tradition identified above?

Explanation: As foreign conquerors, the Manchu Qing rulers heavily patronized Confucianism and Chinese traditions to gain the acceptance and cooperation of the Han Chinese scholar-bureaucrats and populace.

39. The emphasis on "agriculture and sericulture" reflects the traditional Chinese view that:

Explanation: Confucian ideology held agriculture in high esteem as the foundation of a stable state, while merchants were often viewed with suspicion as parasites who profited from others' labor.

40. In addition to issuing edicts like the one above, how did the Qing Dynasty practically administer its vast empire?

Explanation: The Qing maintained and expanded the traditional Confucian civil service exam system to recruit a loyal and educated bureaucracy to administer the empire.

Excerpt from a report by a Spanish colonial official regarding the Potosí silver mines, c. 1590:

"The wealth extracted from the mountain of Potosí is truly staggering, filling the galleons that sail back to Spain. However, this wealth is built upon the mita system, a draft of Indian labor. Villages are required to send a quota of men to toil in the dangerous and toxic depths of the mines for months at a time. The death toll from cave-ins, exhaustion, and mercury poisoning is devastating the indigenous communities of the Andes."

Source: Fictionalized composite based on historical Spanish administrative reports from Peru.

41. The mita system described in the passage was an adaptation of a labor system originally developed by which pre-Columbian civilization?

Explanation: The Spanish adapted the traditional Incan mita system (originally a system of labor tax for public works) into a brutal forced labor system to extract silver from mines like Potosí.

42. The silver extracted from Potosí had which of the following massive macroeconomic effects in Europe?

Explanation: The massive influx of silver into European markets drastically increased the money supply, leading to significant inflation known as the Price Revolution.

43. A large portion of the silver extracted from Potosí eventually ended up in which of the following regions due to global trade imbalances?

Explanation: Because European goods were generally not desired in Asia, Europeans used American silver to pay for Asian luxury goods; additionally, China's requirement that taxes be paid in silver created a massive sink for global silver.

44. The brutal conditions described in the passage were a primary driver for which of the following demographic changes in the Americas?

Explanation: The harsh conditions of the mita, combined with epidemic diseases, caused a severe demographic collapse among the indigenous populations of the Andes.

Excerpt from the charter of the Dutch East India Company (VOC), 1602:

"The States General of the United Netherlands grants this Company the exclusive monopoly to trade in the waters east of the Cape of Good Hope. Furthermore, the Company is granted the sovereign power to maintain armed forces, build fortresses, mint its own coinage, and conclude treaties with Asian monarchs in the name of the Republic."

Source: Adapted from the original VOC Charter of 1602.

45. The powers granted to the VOC in the passage demonstrate which of the following features of early modern capitalism?

Explanation: Joint-stock companies like the VOC acted essentially as sovereign extensions of the state, using military force to secure trade monopolies and maximize profits for shareholders.

46. Which of the following regions became the primary focus of the VOC's monopolistic and militaristic trade policies?

Explanation: The VOC brutally established a monopoly over the highly lucrative spice trade (nutmeg, cloves) in the Indonesian archipelago.

47. How did the Dutch approach to empire-building in Asia differ from the Spanish approach in the Americas?

Explanation: The Dutch established a "trading post empire," prioritizing commercial dominance over trade routes and commodities rather than the massive territorial and cultural conquests seen in Spanish America.

48. The financial innovations that supported companies like the VOC led to the rise of which of the following in Europe?

Explanation: The need to raise massive capital and manage risk for global voyages led to the creation of joint-stock companies, stock exchanges (like the Amsterdam Bourse), and modern banking.

Excerpt from a letter sent by the Ottoman Sultan Selim I to the Safavid Shah Ismail I, 1514:

"I, the sovereign and the executioner of the decrees of Destiny... address myself to you, Amir Ismail. You have subjected the upright community of Muhammad to your heretical whims. You have destroyed the altars of the orthodox believers and raised the banner of schism. Therefore, it is a sacred duty for my armies to march against you, to eradicate your deviant Shi'a sect, and to restore the true Sunni faith to the lands you have unjustly occupied."

Source: Translated correspondence before the Battle of Chaldiran.

49. The conflict described in the passage was primarily fueled by which of the following?

Explanation: The Ottoman-Safavid rivalry was deeply rooted in the religious divide between the staunchly Sunni Ottomans and the vigorously Shi'a Safavids.

50. What was a major political consequence of the religious rivalry expressed in this letter?

Explanation: The religious and territorial rivalry led to centuries of intermittent warfare (such as the Battle of Chaldiran), ultimately resulting in a stable border roughly corresponding to modern-day Turkey and Iran/Iraq.
