Excerpt from Marco Polo's "The Travels of Marco Polo" (circa 1300):

"Samarkand is a very great and splendid city... It has fine and extensive orchards and gardens, and many fine suburbs like those of Damascus. The inhabitants, who are partly Christians and partly Saracens, are subject to the dominion of a nephew of the Great Khan; his city is on the borders of Persia. Here is produced much silk; they have mulberry trees in great abundance, on which the worms that spin silk feed."

Source: Marco Polo, The Travels of Marco Polo, translated by Ronald Latham, Penguin Books, 1958 (as cited in https://www.advantour.com/silkroad/marco-polo.htm).

1. Based on the passage, the city of Samarkand's significance in the Silk Roads was primarily due to

The passage emphasizes Samarkand's orchards, gardens, and silk production, highlighting its economic role in facilitating trade along the Silk Roads.

2. The mention of both Christians and Saracens in the passage reflects which aspect of the Silk Roads during the period c. 1200–1450?

The passage notes a mixed population of Christians and Muslims (Saracens) under Mongol dominion, illustrating the multicultural nature of Silk Road hubs.

3. Marco Polo’s description of Samarkand’s location "on the borders of Persia" suggests that the city was important for

Samarkand's border location positioned it as a key intermediary point for goods moving along the Silk Roads between Persia and other regions.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of the Silk Roads, as described in the passage, during the period c. 1200–1450?

Explanation: The passage highlights silk production, diverse populations, and abundant merchandise, reflecting the Silk Roads' role in exchanging goods and fostering cultural interactions.

Excerpt from Ibn Battuta's "Rihla" (circa 1355):

"We traveled thence to the city of Qaliqut [Calicut], which is one of the chief ports in Mulaibar. It is visited by men from China, Jawa, Ceylon, the Maldives, al-Yaman and Fars, and in it gather merchants from all quarters. The harbor is one of the largest in the world. We anchored at a distance of two farsakhs from the port, and were met by small boats called ajwaf in which we entered the harbor."

Source: Ibn Battuta, The Travels of Ibn Battuta, translated by H.A.R. Gibb, Cambridge University Press, 1958 (as cited in https://bridgingcultures-muslimjourneys.org/items/show/84).

1. Based on the passage, the port of Calicut was significant in the Indian Ocean trade because it

The passage lists visitors from China, Jawa, Ceylon, Maldives, Yemen, and Persia, highlighting Calicut's role as a multicultural trade hub.

2. The description of Calicut's harbor as "one of the largest in the world" suggests that the Indian Ocean network facilitated

The large harbor and gathering of merchants indicate the expansion of port cities driven by Indian Ocean commerce.

3. Ibn Battuta’s mention of small boats (ajwaf) for entering the harbor reflects which aspect of Indian Ocean trade?

The need for small boats to anchor larger vessels shows practical adaptations to harbor conditions in facilitating trade.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of Indian Ocean exchange, as described in the passage, during the period c. 1200–1450?

Explanation: The diverse origins of merchants demonstrate the network's role in connecting and exchanging ideas and goods across regions.

Excerpt from Al-Bakri's "Book of Routes and Realms" (circa 1068):

"The town of Ghiyaru is twelve miles from the [Niger River] and contains many Muslims. It is a great center of commerce, and caravans come there from all parts of the Sudan. The gold is found in the ground in nuggets, and is dug out by the people. The king takes the nuggets, but the gold dust is left to the people."

Source: Al-Bakri, Book of Routes and Realms, translated by Nehemia Levtzion and J.F.P. Hopkins, Corpus of Early Arabic Sources for West African History, Cambridge University Press, 1981 (as cited in https://wasscehistorytextbook.com/2-trans-saharan-trade-origins-organization-and-effects-in-the-development-of-west-africa/).

1. Based on the passage, the town of Ghiyaru was important in the Trans-Saharan trade primarily for

The passage emphasizes Ghiyaru as a commerce center with gold trade and caravans from the Sudan.

2. The king's control over gold nuggets, as described in the passage, reflects which aspect of Trans-Saharan trade?

The king taking nuggets while leaving dust to the people shows control over high-value gold to consolidate power.

3. Al-Bakri’s mention of "many Muslims" in Ghiyaru suggests that Trans-Saharan trade contributed to

The presence of Muslims in a trade center indicates Islam's diffusion through trade networks.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of Trans-Saharan trade, as described in the passage, during the period c. 1200–1450?

Explanation: Caravans from the Sudan and gold commerce highlight the network's role in connecting regions economically.

Excerpt from Xuanzang's "Record of the Western Regions" (circa 646):

"In my travels along the Silk Roads, I observed Buddhist monks from India sharing sutras in the markets of Samarkand and Chang’an. Merchants and pilgrims carry these teachings, building stupas and monasteries in distant lands. Scholars also exchange knowledge of mathematics and astronomy, learned from Indian texts, which are studied alongside Islamic learning in the cities of Central Asia."

Source: Xuanzang, Record of the Western Regions, translated by Samuel Beal, Buddhist Records of the Western World, Trübner & Co., 1884 (as cited in https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/content/spread-disease-along-silk-roads).

1. Based on the passage, the diffusion of Buddhism along the Silk Roads was facilitated by

The passage describes monks, merchants, and pilgrims carrying teachings and constructing religious sites.

2. The exchange of mathematical and astronomical knowledge in the passage reflects

Scholars study Indian texts alongside Islamic learning, indicating syncretic cultural diffusion.

3. Xuanzang’s observation of stupas and monasteries in distant lands suggests that cultural connectivity led to

Building stupas and monasteries in distant lands shows the spread of Buddhist architecture and practice.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of cultural diffusion, as described in the passage, during the period c. 1200–1450?

Explanation: The sharing of sutras, mathematics, and astronomy highlights the exchange of knowledge via trade routes.

Excerpt from "The Journey of William of Rubruck" (circa 1255):

"Along the Silk Roads, I saw merchants carrying rice and bananas from the East to the markets of Central Asia, where they are prized for their nourishment. Yet, with the caravans come diseases, for a great plague has struck the cities of the Mongols, spreading swiftly to Persia and beyond. The dead are many, and trade slows where the sickness rages."

Source: William of Rubruck, The Journey of William of Rubruck to the Eastern Parts of the World, 1253–55, translated by William Woodville Rockhill, Hakluyt Society, 1900 (as cited in https://www.history.com/articles/silk-road-black-death).

1. Based on the passage, the diffusion of crops like rice and bananas along trade routes contributed to

The passage notes these crops are prized for nourishment in Central Asian markets, indicating positive environmental and dietary impacts.

2. The "great plague" mentioned in the passage most likely refers to

The description of a plague striking Mongol cities and spreading to Persia aligns with the Black Death's path via Silk Roads.

3. According to the passage, the spread of diseases along caravans had which effect on trade?

The passage states "trade slows where the sickness rages," linking disease to disruptions in commerce.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of connectivity on environments, as described in the passage, during the period c. 1200–1450?

Explanation: The spread of nourishing crops and deadly plagues via caravans shows dual positive and negative environmental consequences.
