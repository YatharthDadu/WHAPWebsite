Excerpt from Christopher Columbus's Journal (1492):

I presented them with some red caps, and some strings of glass beads, which they hung round their necks, and many other things of little value, with which they were delighted, and became so attached to us that it was a marvel."

Source: Christopher Columbus, Journal of the First Voyage, translated by Clements R. Markham, Hakluyt Society, 1893 (as cited in https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1492/1492-h/1492-h.htm).

Europeans offered low-value manufactured items (enabled by innovations) for delight, highlighting asymmetrical exchanges that favored Europeans.

3. Columbus's description of the indigenous people's attachment as "a marvel" suggests that technological innovations contributed to

The "marvel" at their delight indicates Europeans viewed indigenous reactions through a lens of cultural and technological disparity.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of technological innovations like those enabling Columbus's voyage during the period c. 1450–1750?

Explanation: Navigational advancements led to transoceanic voyages, fostering exchanges of goods, ideas, and diseases across the world.

Excerpt from Vasco da Gama's Report to the King of Portugal (1498):

"We departed from the river of Bonaventura on the 24th of January, and shaped our course for the island of Sam Rafael, where we remained three days cleaning our ships and taking in water and wood. On the 1st of February we set sail, steering for the open sea, and on the 3rd we sighted land, which was the coast of the land of the good people, as it is called by the natives."

Source: Vasco da Gama, A Journal of the First Voyage of Vasco da Gama, translated by E.G. Ravenstein, Hakluyt Society, 1898 (as cited in https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1493/1493-h/1493-h.htm).

2. The passage’s mention of "cleaning our ships and taking in water and wood" highlights which challenge of exploration in this period?

Stopping for supplies underscores the practical demands of sustaining extended sea journeys.

3. Da Gama's reference to "the land of the good people" suggests that European explorers often

The term reflects European naming and perceptions, often romanticized or dismissive of local identities.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of explorations like da Gama's during the period c. 1450–1750?

Explanation: Da Gama's route to India opened direct trade, leading to Portuguese dominance in the Indian Ocean and global commerce.

Excerpt from the Treaty of Tordesillas (1494):

"Forasmuch as between the said lords, their constituents, there has been a difference over the lands and seas which the said King of Portugal has discovered and which the said Sovereigns of Castile claim belong to them... It is hereby agreed that a line be drawn from pole to pole, 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde Islands, and that all lands to the east belong to Portugal, and to the west to Castile."

Source: Treaty of Tordesillas, 1494, as cited in "The European Discovery of America: The Southern Voyages" by Samuel Eliot Morison, Oxford University Press, 1974 (accessed via https://avalon.law.yale.edu/15th_century/mod001.asp).

1. Based on the passage, the Treaty of Tordesillas was primarily intended to

The treaty draws a line to allocate eastern lands to Portugal and western to Spain (Castile), resolving colonial disputes.

2. The "difference over the lands and seas" mentioned in the passage reflects which cause of maritime empire establishment?

The dispute over discoveries highlights rivalry driving maritime expansion and treaty negotiations.

3. The line "from pole to pole" in the treaty suggests that maritime empires were established through

The meridian line, endorsed by the Pope, divided the world, establishing spheres for Spanish and Portuguese empires.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of treaties like Tordesillas during the period c. 1450–1750?

Explanation: The treaty legitimized divisions that enabled extensive Spanish and Portuguese empires in the Americas and beyond.

Explanation: Discoveries and trade monopolies transformed world commerce, positioning European powers centrally.

Excerpt from Olaudah Equiano's "The Interesting Narrative" (1789):

"I was soon put down under the decks, and there I received such a salutation in my nostrils as I had never experienced in my life: so that, with the loathsomeness of the stench, and crying together, I became so sick and low that I was not able to eat... Explanation: The trade entrenched racialized systems, shaping colonial societies with lasting inequalities.
