Excerpt from Zhao Rukuo’s "Description of the Foreign Lands" (circa 1225):

"In the Song Dynasty, the emperor’s court in Hangzhou is adorned with fine silks and ceramics, traded widely with foreign lands. Confucian scholars govern with virtue, and Buddhist temples flourish, supported by imperial grants. The Grand Canal links the capital to northern markets, facilitating the flow of rice and goods, strengthening the realm’s prosperity."

Source: Zhao Rukuo, Description of the Foreign Lands, translated by Friedrich Hirth and W.W. Rockhill, Chau Ju-Kua: His Work on the Chinese and Arab Trade in the Twelfth and Thirteenth Centuries, Imperial Academy of Sciences, 1911 (as cited in https://www.persee.fr/doc/arch_0044-8613_1991_num_41_1_2785).

1. Based on the passage, the Song Dynasty’s governance was primarily characterized by

The passage mentions Confucian scholars governing with virtue, indicating a centralized bureaucratic system rooted in Confucian ideology.

2. The passage’s reference to the Grand Canal suggests it was significant for

The canal is described as linking the capital to northern markets for rice and goods, highlighting its role in economic connectivity.

3. The imperial support for Buddhist temples, as mentioned in the passage, reflects which aspect of Song Dynasty society?

Imperial grants to Buddhist temples suggest state patronage, integrating religious institutions into the broader governance structure.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of developments in the Song Dynasty, as described in the passage, during the period c. 1200–1450?

Explanation: The passage highlights trade, ceramics, and the Grand Canal, showing how the Song Dynasty’s developments enhanced economic and cultural connectivity.

The passage describes blending Greek and Indian knowledge with local innovations, highlighting a syncretic approach to technological development.

2. The use of paper from China, as mentioned in the passage, suggests that Dar al-Islam’s advancements were supported by

Paper from China enabled the recording of algebra, indicating that trade facilitated the spread of technologies critical to scholarship.

3. The mention of water clocks and automata being prized in sultans’ courts suggests that technological advancements contributed to

The devices’ value in sultans’ courts shows their role in elevating cultural status and supporting royal legitimacy through patronage.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of technological advancements in Dar al-Islam, as described in the passage, during the period c. 1200–1450?

Explanation: The passage notes merchants carrying devices and paper-based scholarship, indicating the spread of innovations through trade networks.

Excerpt from Domingo Paes’s Account of Vijayanagara (circa 1520, describing the 14th century):

The Hindu king rules with justice, patronizing temples dedicated to Vishnu and Shiva. Source: Domingo Paes, Chronicle of the Kings of Vijayanagara, translated by Robert Sewell, A Forgotten Empire: Vijayanagara, Swan Sonnenschein & Co., 1900 (as cited in https://www.columbia.edu/itc/mealac/pritchett/00islamlinks/txt_vijayanagara.html).

The passage describes a Hindu king patronizing temples and protecting trade routes, indicating a monarchy that integrated religious and economic roles.

2. The passage’s mention of markets trading spices and gems suggests that Vijayanagara’s economy was significant for

Spices and gems are luxury goods associated with maritime trade, linking Vijayanagara to broader Indian Ocean commerce.

3. The protection of roads by the king’s armies, as described in the passage, contributed to

Protecting roads ensured safe passage for merchants, fostering trade and wealth as described.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of the Vijayanagara Empire, as described in the passage, during the period c. 1200–1450?

Explanation: The passage highlights markets, trade protection, and temple patronage, showing Vijayanagara’s role in advancing trade and Hindu culture.

Excerpt from John Darwin’s "After Tamerlane: The Rise and Fall of Global Empires, 1400–2000" (2008, discussing earlier periods):

"State formation in the 13th century varied widely. In China, the Song Dynasty used Confucian bureaucracy and the Grand Canal to centralize power. The Mali Empire in Africa leveraged gold trade and Islamic governance to unify its regions. In Europe, feudal monarchies like France relied on vassalage and land-based obligations, with local lords wielding significant authority."

Source: John Darwin, After Tamerlane: The Rise and Fall of Global Empires, 1400–2000, Bloomsbury Press, 2008 (as cited in https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/after-tamerlane-9781596916029/).

1. Based on the passage, which of the following best compares the economic foundations of state formation in the Song Dynasty and the Mali Empire?

The passage highlights the Song’s Grand Canal for trade and Mali’s gold trade, distinguishing their economic bases.

2. The passage suggests that a key similarity between the Mali Empire and European feudal monarchies was

Mali’s Islamic governance and Europe’s feudal ties to the Church (implied in vassalage) show cultural legitimacy in state building.

3. According to the passage, how did the Song Dynasty’s state formation differ from that of European feudal monarchies?

The passage contrasts the Song’s centralized Confucian system with Europe’s decentralized feudal structure.

4. Which of the following best illustrates a broader similarity in state formation across the Song, Mali, and European systems, as described in the passage?

Explanation: The passage highlights economic systems (canals, gold trade, land obligations) and cultural elements (Confucianism, Islam, feudal ties) in state formation.
