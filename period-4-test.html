Excerpt from Vladimir Lenin's "April Theses," 1917:

"The specific feature of the present situation in Russia is that the country is passing from the first stage of the revolution—which, owing to the insufficient class-consciousness and organization of the proletariat, placed power in the hands of the bourgeoisie—to its second stage, which must place power in the hands of the proletariat and the poorest sections of the peasants... The masses must be made to see that the Soviets of Workers' Deputies are the only possible form of revolutionary government."

Source: Vladimir Lenin, "The Tasks of the Proletariat in the Present Revolution" (April Theses), 1917.

1. Which of the following best describes the historical context of Lenin's arguments in the excerpt?

Explanation: Lenin wrote the April Theses in 1917, following the February Revolution that overthrew the tsar amidst the military failures and economic crises exacerbated by Russia's involvement in World War I.

2. Lenin's call to place power in the hands of the "poorest sections of the peasants" most directly reflects which of the following?

Explanation: Traditional Marxism expected revolution in highly industrialized societies, but Lenin adapted this for Russia, where the industrial proletariat was small and the peasantry made up the vast majority.

3. The political shifts advocated by Lenin in the excerpt most directly led to which of the following outcomes?

Explanation: Lenin's arguments provided the ideological foundation for the Bolsheviks to seize power in the October Revolution of 1917, ultimately leading to the formation of the USSR.

4. Which of the following subsequent Soviet policies most directly contradicted the empowering of the peasantry suggested in the excerpt?

Explanation: While Lenin promised power to the peasants, Stalin's later policy of forced collectivization stripped peasants of their land, leading to widespread famine and loss of autonomy.

Excerpt from a memorandum by Eyre Crowe, British Foreign Office, 1907:

"The general character of England's foreign policy is determined by the immutable conditions of her geographical situation on the ocean flank of Europe as an island State with vast oversea colonies and dependencies... It would be but natural that the power of a State supreme at sea should inspire universal jealousy and fear... In order to reconcile the rest of the world to an overwhelmingly strong British navy, it must therefore be the policy of Great Britain to harmonize with the general desires and ideals common to all mankind, and more particularly that it should be closely identified with the primary and vital interests of a majority... of the other nations."

Source: Eyre Crowe, Memorandum on the Present State of British Relations with France and Germany, January 1, 1907.

5. Crowe's emphasis on Britain's "vast oversea colonies and dependencies" best highlights which of the following long-term causes of World War I?

Explanation: The passage references Britain's global empire, highlighting the intense imperial rivalry that created tensions among European states prior to World War I.

6. The "universal jealousy and fear" mentioned in the excerpt most directly relates to which of the following developments in the early 20th century?

Explanation: Britain's naval supremacy and Germany's aggressive efforts to build a competing navy created significant anxiety and an arms race that contributed to the outbreak of the war.

7. The memorandum suggests that Britain should align its interests with other nations, which most directly contributed to which of the following?

Explanation: Crowe's argument that Britain needed to identify with the interests of other nations reflects the strategic thinking that led to alliances like the Triple Entente to counterbalance the threat from the Triple Alliance.

8. Which of the following events would most directly challenge Crowe's assertion that British naval power could be reconciled with the "ideals common to all mankind"?

Explanation: The blockade was a highly controversial use of naval power that caused immense civilian suffering, challenging the idea that British naval supremacy harmonized with universal ideals.

Excerpt from Franklin D. Roosevelt's First Inaugural Address, 1933:

"Our greatest primary task is to put people to work. This is no unsolvable problem if we face it wisely and courageously. It can be accomplished in part by direct recruiting by the Government itself, treating the task as we would treat the emergency of a war, but at the same time, through this employment, accomplishing greatly needed projects to stimulate and reorganize the use of our natural resources... Finally, in our progress toward a resumption of work we require two safeguards against a return of the evils of the old order; there must be a strict supervision of all banking and credits and investments; there must be an end to speculation with other people's money."

Source: Franklin D. Roosevelt, First Inaugural Address, March 4, 1933.

9. Roosevelt's proposed policies in the excerpt represent a significant shift from which of the following prevailing economic philosophies?

Explanation: The New Deal policies advocated by Roosevelt involved significant government intervention in the economy, departing from the hands-off, laissez-faire approach that characterized the 1920s.

10. The economic conditions that prompted Roosevelt's address were primarily a consequence of which of the following?

Explanation: Roosevelt was responding to the Great Depression, which was triggered by the 1929 stock market crash and resulted in catastrophic global economic collapse.

11. Roosevelt's call for government action to "put people to work" is most similar in its economic approach to which of the following contemporary developments?

Explanation: While operating in very different political systems, both the New Deal and the Soviet Five-Year Plans utilized extensive government intervention and planning to manage and stimulate the economy during the interwar period.

12. The policies referenced in the excerpt had which of the following long-term effects on the role of the government in the United States?

Explanation: The New Deal established a lasting legacy of government involvement in providing a social safety net and regulating the economy in the United States.

Excerpt from the Hossbach Memorandum, a summary of a meeting between Adolf Hitler and his military leadership, 1937:

"The aim of German policy was to make secure and to preserve the racial community and to enlarge it. It was therefore a question of space... The German future was therefore dependent exclusively on the solution of the need for space... The history of all ages—the Roman Empire and the British Empire—had proved that expansion could only be carried out by breaking down resistance and taking risks... The question for Germany ran: where could she achieve the greatest gain at the lowest cost."

Source: Hossbach Memorandum, November 5, 1937.

13. Hitler's arguments in the excerpt most directly reflect which of the following ideologies?

Explanation: The excerpt highlights the core fascist tenets of extreme nationalism, racial superiority ("racial community"), and the need for aggressive territorial expansion (Lebensraum).

14. The concept of "space" mentioned in the memorandum (Lebensraum) was primarily intended to be acquired at the expense of which of the following?

Explanation: Hitler's vision for Lebensraum specifically targeted Eastern Europe and the Soviet Union to provide agricultural land and resources for the German "master race."

15. Which of the following actions taken by the international community in the 1930s allowed Hitler to pursue the expansionist goals outlined in the excerpt?

Explanation: Britain and France's policy of appeasement, hoping to avoid another war by conceding to Hitler's early territorial demands, emboldened Germany to continue its aggressive expansion.

16. The comparison to the "Roman Empire and the British Empire" suggests that Hitler viewed his expansionist aims as:

Explanation: Hitler used the historical precedents of successful empires to legitimize his own aggressive pursuit of territory and dominance.

Excerpt from the Yalta Conference Agreement, February 1945:

"The establishment of order in Europe and the rebuilding of national economic life must be achieved by processes which will enable the liberated peoples to destroy the last vestiges of Nazism and Fascism and to create democratic institutions of their own choice. This is a principle of the Atlantic Charter—the right of all peoples to choose the form of government under which they will live... To foster the conditions in which the liberated peoples may exercise these rights, the three governments will jointly assist the people in any European liberated state or former Axis satellite state in Europe... to form interim governmental authorities broadly representative of all democratic elements in the population and pledged to the earliest possible establishment through free elections of governments responsive to the will of the people."

Source: Protocol of Proceedings of Crimea Conference (Yalta), February 11, 1945.

17. The principles regarding "free elections" outlined in the excerpt became a major source of tension between which of the following powers shortly after the conference?

Explanation: The Soviet Union's failure to allow truly free elections in Eastern Europe, instead installing pro-Soviet communist regimes, was a primary catalyst for the Cold War between the US and the USSR.

18. The reference to the "right of all peoples to choose the form of government" (self-determination) inadvertently fueled which of the following post-WWII movements?

Explanation: While the Allied powers intended self-determination primarily for liberated European states, colonized peoples worldwide seized upon this rhetoric to demand their own independence from imperial rule.

19. Which of the following events best demonstrates the failure of the agreement's promise to "create democratic institutions" in Eastern Europe?

Explanation: The Soviet-backed communist takeover of Czechoslovakia explicitly violated the Yalta promise of democratic institutions and free elections in liberated nations.

20. The Yalta agreement's focus on destroying the "last vestiges of Nazism" was most directly implemented through which of the following actions?

Explanation: The Nuremberg Trials were the judicial mechanism used by the Allies to systematically dismantle the Nazi leadership and hold them accountable for war crimes.

Excerpt from President Harry S. Truman's Address to Congress (The Truman Doctrine), 1947:

"At the present moment in world history nearly every nation must choose between alternative ways of life. The choice is too often not a free one. One way of life is based upon the will of the majority, and is distinguished by free institutions, representative government, free elections, guarantees of individual liberty, freedom of speech and religion, and freedom from political oppression. The second way of life is based upon the will of a minority forcibly imposed upon the majority. It relies upon terror and oppression, a controlled press and radio, fixed elections, and the suppression of personal freedoms. I believe that it must be the policy of the United States to support free peoples who are resisting attempted subjugation by armed minorities or by outside pressures."

Source: Harry S. Truman, Address to a Joint Session of Congress, March 12, 1947.

21. Truman's description of the "second way of life" was a direct characterization of the political system of which of the following nations?

Explanation: Truman was explicitly describing the totalitarian communist system of the Soviet Union to rally domestic support for opposing Soviet expansion.

22. The policy of supporting "free peoples who are resisting attempted subjugation" came to be known as which of the following?

Explanation: The Truman Doctrine established the US policy of containment, aiming to stop the spread of communism anywhere in the world by providing military and economic aid.

23. Which of the following conflicts was the most immediate application of the policy outlined in the excerpt?

Explanation: Truman requested this aid specifically to support the Greek government against a communist insurgency and to bolster Turkey against Soviet pressure, making it the first major application of the doctrine.

24. The worldview presented in the excerpt most directly contributed to which of the following broader global trends during the Cold War?

Explanation: The stark, binary worldview ("nearly every nation must choose") articulated by Truman defined the bipolar nature of the Cold War, forcing nations to align with either the US or the USSR.

Excerpt from Mao Zedong's "On the People's Democratic Dictatorship," 1949:

"The experiences of the Chinese movement... have taught us to lean to one side, and we are firmly convinced that in order to win victory and consolidate it we must lean to one side... Not only in China but also in the world, without exception, one either leans to the side of imperialism or to the side of socialism. Neutrality is a camouflage and a third road does not exist... We belong to the anti-imperialist front headed by the Soviet Union, and so we can turn only to this side for genuine and friendly help."

Source: Mao Zedong, Commemorating the 28th Anniversary of the Communist Party of China, June 30, 1949.

25. Mao's statement that China must "lean to one side" reflects which of the following realities of the early Cold War era?

Explanation: Mao recognized that the world was divided into two superpowers and their respective blocs, and concluded that China must align entirely with the Soviet (socialist) bloc.

26. The assertion that "a third road does not exist" was a direct repudiation of the goals of which of the following later groups?

Explanation: The Non-Aligned Movement, formed later by leaders like Nehru and Sukarno, explicitly attempted to forge a "third road" independent of both the US and the USSR, a concept Mao rejected here.

27. The alliance between China and the Soviet Union referenced in the excerpt would eventually be strained by which of the following?

Explanation: By the 1960s, the Sino-Soviet split occurred largely due to ideological disputes over Marxist doctrine, peaceful coexistence with the West, and competition for leadership of the global communist movement.

28. Mao's rise to power and the establishment of the People's Republic of China in 1949 was most directly facilitated by which of the following?

Explanation: The Chinese Communist Party won the civil war primarily by mobilizing the vast peasant population with promises of land reform, capitalizing on the exhaustion caused by WWII.

Excerpt from Kwame Nkrumah's speech at the foundation of the Organization of African Unity (OAU), 1963:

"African unity is, above all, a political kingdom which can only be gained by political means. The social and economic development of Africa will come only within the political kingdom, not the other way round... No single African State is large or powerful enough to stand on its own... So long as we remain balkanized, regionally or territorially, we shall be at the mercy of colonialism and imperialism... The resources are there. It is for us to marshal them in the active service of our people. Unless we do this by our concerted efforts, within the framework of our combined planning, we shall not progress at the tempo demanded by today's events and the mood of our people."

Source: Kwame Nkrumah, Speech at the Addis Ababa Summit Conference, May 24, 1963.

29. Nkrumah's call for "African unity" and his warning against remaining "balkanized" most directly reflect the ideology of:

Explanation: Nkrumah was a leading proponent of Pan-Africanism, which advocated for the political and economic unification of the African continent to ensure true independence.

30. The fear of being "at the mercy of colonialism and imperialism" even after achieving formal independence describes the concept of:

Explanation: Neocolonialism refers to the continued economic and political dominance of former colonial powers or new superpowers over newly independent nations, a major concern for leaders like Nkrumah.

31. Nkrumah's argument that "social and economic development... will come only within the political kingdom" suggests which of the following about his approach to decolonization?

Explanation: Nkrumah believed that true economic development was impossible without strong, united political sovereignty free from outside interference.

32. Which of the following factors most hindered the realization of the unified Africa envisioned by Nkrumah in the late 20th century?

Explanation: The borders established during the Scramble for Africa created internal ethnic conflicts and instability within independent nations, making continental unity incredibly difficult.

Excerpt from the Constitution of India, adopted in 1949:

"WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:

JUSTICE, social, economic and political;

LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;

EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;

and to promote among them all

FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation..."

Source: Preamble to the Constitution of India.

33. The emphasis on making India a "SECULAR" republic in the preamble was primarily a response to which of the following historical realities?

Explanation: The brutal religious violence that accompanied the partition of India and Pakistan in 1947 led India's founders to emphasize a secular state to protect its diverse religious minorities and prevent further communal conflict.

34. The preamble's commitment to "JUSTICE, social, economic and political" and "EQUALITY of status" most directly challenged which of the following long-standing social structures in India?

Explanation: The constitutional promise of social equality and justice was a direct effort to legally dismantle the inequalities enforced by the traditional caste system, including the practice of untouchability.

35. The phrase "WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA" reflects the influence of which of the following intellectual traditions?

Explanation: The concept of popular sovereignty—that political power is derived from the people—is a core tenet of Enlightenment thought that influenced many independence movements and constitutions.

36. The subsequent political trajectory of India after adopting this constitution differed most significantly from newly independent nations in sub-Saharan Africa in that India:

Explanation: Unlike many newly independent nations that fell into military dictatorships or one-party rule, India managed to sustain a large-scale electoral democracy.

37. The protests depicted in the image were part of a global wave of unrest in 1968 that generally protested against which of the following?

Explanation: The global protests of 1968, including those in Paris, were driven by youth and workers disillusioned with the conservative post-war establishment, consumer capitalism, and imperialist conflicts like the Vietnam War.

38. The alliance between "Workers and Students" mentioned in the banners reflects a synthesis of which two political traditions?

Explanation: The May 1968 protests were notable for the temporary alignment of traditional labor unions (focused on wages and working conditions) and radical students (focused on social reform and anti-imperialism).

39. Which of the following movements in the United States during the 1960s was most ideologically similar to the events shown in the photograph?

Explanation: The student and worker protests in France shared the anti-establishment, pro-civil rights, and anti-war sentiments of the contemporaneous movements in the United States.

40. While the protests in Paris did not result in a revolution, they had which of the following long-term cultural impacts in Western societies?

Explanation: Although they failed politically to overthrow the government, the 1968 protests catalyzed major cultural shifts in Western Europe, challenging traditional authority and accelerating social liberalism.

Excerpt from a report by Norman Borlaug, agronomist and "Father of the Green Revolution," 1970:

"The Green Revolution has won a temporary success in man's war against hunger and deprivation; it has given man a breathing space. If fully implemented, the revolution can provide sufficient food for sustenance during the next three decades. But the frightening power of human reproduction must also be curbed; otherwise the success of the Green Revolution will be ephemeral only... We are dealing with two opposing forces, the scientific power of food production and the biologic power of human reproduction... There can be no permanent progress in the battle against hunger until the agencies that fight for increased food production and those that fight for population control unite in a common effort."

Source: Norman Borlaug, Nobel Peace Prize Acceptance Speech, December 11, 1970.

41. The "Green Revolution" mentioned by Borlaug primarily involved which of the following technological advancements?

Explanation: The Green Revolution was characterized by the introduction of scientifically bred, high-yield crop varieties (especially wheat and rice) and the heavy use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides.

42. Borlaug's concern about the "frightening power of human reproduction" highlights which of the following demographic trends of the 20th century?

Explanation: Improved medicine (like antibiotics and vaccines) and the increased food supply from the Green Revolution led to unprecedented exponential population growth in the 20th century.

43. Which of the following was a significant negative environmental consequence of the agricultural practices promoted by the Green Revolution?

Explanation: While the Green Revolution vastly increased crop yields, its reliance on chemical inputs caused significant long-term environmental damage, including nutrient runoff and soil depletion.

44. The success of the Green Revolution had which of the following economic impacts on rural societies in the developing world?

Explanation: Because the new farming methods required expensive inputs (seeds, fertilizer, irrigation equipment), poorer farmers often couldn't compete, leading to increased rural inequality and corporate consolidation of farmland.

Excerpt from an article by economist Milton Friedman, 1970:

"There is one and only one social responsibility of business—to use its resources and engage in activities designed to increase its profits so long as it stays within the rules of the game, which is to say, engages in open and free competition without deception or fraud... The businessmen believe that they are defending free enterprise when they declaim that business is not concerned 'merely' with profit but also with promoting desirable 'social' ends... In fact they are—or would be if they or anyone else took them seriously—preaching pure and unadulterated socialism. Businessmen who talk this way are unwitting puppets of the intellectual forces that have been undermining the basis of a free society these past decades."

Source: Milton Friedman, "The Social Responsibility of Business is to Increase its Profits," The New York Times Magazine, September 13, 1970.

45. Friedman's argument in the excerpt is a foundational text for which of the following late-20th-century economic shifts?

Explanation: Friedman was a leading figure of the Chicago School of Economics, which advocated for deregulation, privatization, and maximizing shareholder value, core tenets of neoliberalism.

46. The policies advocated by Friedman were most enthusiastically implemented in the 1980s by the governments of which of the following leaders?

Explanation: Reagan in the US and Thatcher in the UK aggressively implemented neoliberal policies, including tax cuts, deregulation, and reducing the power of labor unions.

47. Friedman's rejection of corporate "social responsibility" most directly conflicts with the goals of which of the following modern movements?

Explanation: ESG criteria demand that corporations account for their environmental and social impact, directly opposing Friedman's view that maximizing profit is a corporation's sole duty.

48. Which of the following was a primary consequence of the global adoption of the free-market principles described in the excerpt?

Explanation: Neoliberal policies and free trade encouraged multinational corporations to offshore manufacturing to countries with cheaper labor and fewer regulations to maximize profits.

Excerpt from the United Nations Universal Declaration of Human Rights, 1948:

"Article 1. All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights...

Article 2. Everyone is entitled to all the rights and freedoms set forth in this Declaration, without distinction of any kind, such as race, colour, sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, property, birth or other status...

Article 5. No one shall be subjected to torture or to cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment."

Source: United Nations General Assembly, Universal Declaration of Human Rights, 1948.

49. The drafting of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights was most directly a response to which of the following historical events?

Explanation: The horrific human rights abuses of WWII, particularly the Holocaust, prompted the international community to establish a universal standard of rights to prevent such events from occurring again.

50. The principles articulated in Article 2 provided a moral and legal framework that supported which of the following late-20th-century movements?

Explanation: The declaration's explicit prohibition of discrimination based on race provided a powerful international justification for opposing the legalized racial segregation of apartheid.

51. Despite the widespread adoption of the Declaration, its enforcement has frequently been limited by which of the following concepts in international relations?

Explanation: The UN has limited power to enforce human rights within the borders of sovereign nations, as states often argue that international intervention violates their domestic jurisdiction.

52. The inclusion of "sex" as a prohibited basis for discrimination in Article 2 reflects the growing influence of which of the following?

Explanation: The explicit protection of women's rights in the UN charter reflected the successes and continued advocacy of international feminist organizations in the mid-20th century.

Excerpt from a manifesto by the Zapatista Army of National Liberation (EZLN), Mexico, 1994:

"We are a product of 500 years of struggle... But today, we say ENOUGH IS ENOUGH. We are the heirs of the true builders of our nation... The North American Free Trade Agreement is the death certificate for the indigenous peoples of Mexico, who are despised and ignored by the government of our country... We have nothing to lose, absolutely nothing, no decent roof over our heads, no land, no work, no poor health, no food, no education, no right to freely and democratically choose our leaders."

Source: First Declaration of the Lacandon Jungle, EZLN, January 1, 1994.

53. The Zapatistas' timing of their uprising to coincide with the implementation of NAFTA highlights their opposition to which of the following?

Explanation: The Zapatistas rose up on the exact day NAFTA went into effect, viewing the free trade agreement as a tool of neoliberalism that would destroy indigenous agricultural livelihoods by flooding the market with cheap foreign goods.

54. The reference to being a "product of 500 years of struggle" connects the Zapatista movement to which of the following historical continuities?

Explanation: The manifesto explicitly links the modern struggle against globalization to the historical struggle of indigenous peoples against Spanish conquest and centuries of marginalization by the Mexican state.

55. The grievances listed by the Zapatistas (no land, no work, no health) best illustrate which of the following criticisms of late-20th-century globalization?

Explanation: Critics of globalization argue that while it increases overall wealth, the benefits are unevenly distributed, often devastating traditional agricultural communities and increasing poverty among marginalized groups.

56. Which of the following tactics made the Zapatista movement distinct from earlier 20th-century peasant revolts?

Explanation: The Zapatistas are often considered the first "post-modern" revolutionary movement because they effectively used the internet and international media to bypass state censorship and build global support for their cause.
