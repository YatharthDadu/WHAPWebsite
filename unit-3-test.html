Excerpt from Suleiman the Magnificent's Letter to King Ferdinand I (1526):

"I who am the Sultan of Sultans, the sovereign of sovereigns, the dispenser of crowns to the monarchs on the face of the earth, the shadow of God on earth, the Sultan and sovereign lord of the White Sea and of the Black Sea, of Rumelia and of Anatolia, of Karamania, of the land of Romans, of Dhulkadria, of Diyarbakir, of Kurdistan, of Azerbaijan, of Persia, of Damascus, of Aleppo, of Cairo, of Mecca, of Medina, of Jerusalem, of all Arabia, of Yemen and of many other lands which my noble forefathers and my glorious ancestors (may God light up their tombs!) conquered by the force of their arms and which my August Majesty has made subject to my flamboyant sword and my victorious blade, I, Sultan Suleiman Khan..."

Source: Suleiman the Magnificent, Letter to Ferdinand I, 1526, as cited in "The Ottoman Empire and the World Around It" by Suraiya Faroqhi, I.B. Tauris, 2004 (accessed via https://www.theottomans.org/english/campaigns_army/1526-suleiman.asp).

1. Based on the passage, Suleiman's self-description as the "Sultan of Sultans" and "shadow of God on earth" primarily illustrates which aspect of Ottoman empire expansion?

Suleiman's titles emphasize divine authority and inheritance from conquerors, reflecting how Ottoman rulers used religious claims to legitimize military expansion.

2. The passage’s reference to lands conquered by "the force of their arms" and "my victorious blade" suggests that Ottoman expansion was driven by

The emphasis on arms and blade symbolizes military conquest, aligning with the Ottoman use of gunpowder weapons for expansion in the period.

3. Suleiman's list of territories, including Cairo, Mecca, and Jerusalem, highlights which outcome of Ottoman expansion?

The mentioned cities represent strategic and religious centers, showing how expansion secured Ottoman dominance in the Islamic world.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of Ottoman empire expansion, as described in the passage, during the period c. 1450–1750?

Explanation: The diverse lands listed demonstrate the Ottoman's ability to expand and integrate various regions, fostering a vast, multicultural empire.

Excerpt from the Ottoman Devshirme System Description (16th century):

"The tribute children are gathered from the Christian subjects of the Sultan in Rumeli, and nowadays from Anatolia as well... They are gathered every 5 years, or 7 years, or sometimes every 3 years, according to the need. The boys are selected for their physical and mental qualities, converted to Islam, and trained for service in the janissary corps or the palace administration."

Source: Evliya Çelebi, Seyahatname (Book of Travels), 17th century, as cited in "The Ottoman Empire: The Classical Age 1300-1600" by Halil Inalcik, Phoenix Press, 2000 (accessed via https://www.britannica.com/topic/devshirme).

1. Based on the passage, the devshirme system in the Ottoman Empire was primarily designed to

The passage describes selecting boys for conversion and training in the janissary corps or palace, ensuring loyalty to the sultan.

2. The periodic gathering of boys "according to the need," as mentioned in the passage, reflects which aspect of Ottoman administration?

The system’s flexibility based on need indicates a centralized mechanism to sustain administrative and military personnel.

3. The conversion of Christian boys to Islam in the devshirme system suggests that Ottoman administration emphasized

Conversion and training assimilated subjects into the Islamic elite, helping manage a multiethnic empire.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of administrative systems like devshirme in land-based empires during the period c. 1450–1750?

Explanation: The devshirme produced loyal, skilled administrators and soldiers, contributing to Ottoman longevity and territorial control.

Excerpt from Martin Luther's 95 Theses (1517):

"When our Lord and Master Jesus Christ said, 'Repent' (Mt 4:17), he willed the entire life of believers to be one of repentance. This word cannot be understood as referring to the sacrament of penance, that is, confession and satisfaction, as administered by the clergy. Yet it does not mean solely inner repentance; such inner repentance is worthless unless it produces various outward mortification of the flesh."

Source: Martin Luther, 95 Theses, 1517, as cited in "The Protestant Reformation" by Hans J. Hillerbrand, Harper Perennial, 2009 (accessed via https://www.luther.de/en/95thesen.html).

1. Based on the passage, Luther's criticism of the "sacrament of penance" primarily challenged which aspect of the Catholic Church?

Luther argues that repentance is not limited to clergy-administered penance, questioning their intermediary role.

2. The passage’s emphasis on "outward mortification of the flesh" suggests that Luther believed true repentance involved

Luther states that inner repentance must produce outward mortification, advocating a holistic approach.

3. Luther's theses, as excerpted, contributed to which development in European belief systems?

The criticism of penance sparked the Reformation, leading to Protestant splits from Catholicism.

4. Which of the following best illustrates the broader impact of changes in belief systems like the Protestant Reformation during the period c. 1450–1750?

Explanation: Luther's ideas led to denominational splits and wars, transforming European religious landscapes.
