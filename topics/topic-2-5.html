Topic 2.5

Cultural Consequences of Connectivity

Learning objectives

  • Explain the intellectual and cultural effects of the various networks of exchange in Afro-Eurasia from c. 1200 to c. 1450.
Progress 0% · Score 0

Networks encouraged the spread and blending of religions: Buddhism into East and Southeast Asia (and decline in some of its Indian heartland), Hinduism and Buddhism in Southeast Asia, Islam across the Indian Ocean and Sahara, and Sufi and Bhakti styles of devotion.

Travelers as evidence

  • Ibn Battuta — legal scholar whose rihla describes Dar al-Islam from West Africa to China.
  • Marco Polo — Venetian at the Yuan court, later read widely in Europe.
  • Margery Kempe — Christian mystic and pilgrim, a window on European devotion and travel.

Literature, medical texts, and scientific ideas also moved. Gunpowder and paper technologies diffused. Cities became cosmopolitan nodes.
