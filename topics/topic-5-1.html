Topic 5.1

The Enlightenment

Learning objectives

  • Explain the intellectual and ideological context in which revolutions swept the Atlantic world from 1750 to 1900.
Progress 0% · Score 0

Enlightenment thinkers applied reason to politics, religion, and society. Locke’s natural rights and consent of the governed, Rousseau’s social contract, Montesquieu’s separation of powers, and Voltaire’s attacks on intolerance provided a vocabulary for later revolutions.

Ideas spread through print, salons, and coffeehouses. They challenged absolutism and inherited privilege but often excluded women, enslaved people, and the propertyless — Mary Wollstonecraft and Olympe de Gouges demanded that rights apply to women. Abolitionist and nationalist thought grew from the same well.
