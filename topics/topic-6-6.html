Causes of Migration in an Interconnected World

Learning objectives

  • Explain how various environmental and economic factors contributed to the development of the global economy and patterns of migration.
Progress 0% · Score 0

Industrial transport (steamship, railroad) made long-distance migration cheaper. Pushes: famine (Irish potato famine), poverty, persecution (Russian pogroms), population pressure. Pulls: wages in the U.S., Argentina, Canada, Australia; plantation and mine labor in the Caribbean, Indian Ocean, and Southeast Asia.

Coerced and semi-coerced flows continued: Atlantic slave trade declined across the 19th century after legal bans, replaced in part by indentured labor from India and China. Convict labor persisted (Australia).
