Exchange in the Indian Ocean

Learning objectives

  • Explain the causes of the growth of networks of exchange after 1200.
  • Explain the effects of the growth of networks of exchange after 1200.
Progress 0% · Score 0

Predictable monsoon winds, improved compass use, larger dhows and junks, and astrolabes supported a booming Indian Ocean network linking East Africa, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, and China.

Muslim merchants were especially prominent, but Hindu, Buddhist, Jewish, and Chinese traders also participated. Diasporic communities settled in ports, spreading culture as well as goods (spices, textiles, ivory, gold, porcelain).

  • Swahili city-states on the East African coast.
  • Gujarat and other Indian commercial centers.
  • Sultanate of Malacca controlling a key strait after Srivijaya’s decline.
  • Yuan/Ming China as a massive market; later Ming treasure voyages under Zheng He (early 1400s) projected power then stopped.

Bulk goods (timber, grain) as well as luxuries moved more easily by sea than by camel. Indian Ocean trade was more about mutual commercial benefit than a single empire’s command.
