The Mongol Empire and the Making of the Modern World

Learning objectives

  • Explain the process of state building and decline in Eurasia over time.
  • Explain how the expansion of empires influenced trade and communication over time.
  • Explain the significance of the Mongol Empire in larger patterns of continuity and change.
Progress 0% · Score 0

Under Chinggis (Genghis) Khan and his heirs, Mongol armies using cavalry, discipline, and terror built the largest land empire in history. After succession struggles it split into khanates: Yuan China, the Ilkhanate (Persia), the Chagatai Khanate, and the Golden Horde (Russia).

Mongols often left local administrators in place, promoted skilled people regardless of origin, and were pragmatically tolerant of religions while remaining a military elite.

The so-called Pax Mongolica made Silk Road travel safer for a time. Diplomats, merchants (including Marco Polo’s fame in Europe), and ideas moved from East Asia to the Mediterranean.

  • Gunpowder, printing, and medical knowledge spread westward.
  • Greco-Islamic science moved around the khanates.
  • The same routes facilitated the Black Death’s Eurasian sweep.
  • Russian princes such as those of Moscow collected tribute for the Golden Horde — a path toward later Muscovite power.
