Topic 1.6

Developments in Europe from c. 1200 to c. 1450

Learning objectives

  • Explain how the beliefs and practices of the predominant religions in Europe affected European society.
  • Explain the causes and consequences of political decentralization in Europe from c. 1200 to c. 1450.
  • Explain the effects of agriculture on social organization in Europe from c. 1200 to c. 1450.

Christianity, especially the Roman Catholic Church in the west and Orthodox Christianity in the Byzantine/Russian east, structured education, law, art, and political legitimacy. The Church collected tithes, ran monasteries and universities, and claimed authority over kings in spiritual matters.

  • Crusades (earlier waves continuing in memory and later conflicts) linked Europe to the eastern Mediterranean.
  • Jewish communities lived as religious minorities, often as urban lenders and artisans, facing periodic persecution and expulsion.
  • Muslim-ruled al-Andalus shrank during the Reconquista; Islamic learning still flowed into Christian Europe.

Europe was politically fragmented compared with Song China or large Islamic empires. Feudal relationships (lords, vassals, fiefs) and manorialism organized land and military service. Monarchs in England, France, and Iberia slowly gained power, but the Holy Roman Empire stayed decentralized.

The Magna Carta (1215) and English Parliament illustrate limits on royal authority. City-states in Italy and the Hanseatic League in the north showed commercial alternatives to purely landed lordship. The Hundred Years’ War (1337–1453) accelerated royal armies and national identity in France and England.

Manorial agriculture used serf labor bound to the land. The three-field system and new plows slowly raised yields. Population grew until the fourteenth century.

The Black Death (from 1347) killed a huge share of Europeans, labor shortages weakened serfdom in some regions, wages rose, and peasant revolts erupted. Gothic cathedrals, universities, and vernacular literature continued a distinctive European culture even amid crisis.
