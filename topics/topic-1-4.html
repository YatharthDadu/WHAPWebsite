Topic 1.4

State Building in the Americas

Learning objectives

  • Explain how and why states in the Americas developed and changed over time.
Progress 0% · Score 0

In the Americas, state systems demonstrated continuity, innovation, and diversity, and expanded in scope and reach. The Mexica (Aztecs) built a tributary empire centered on Tenochtitlan in the Valley of Mexico.

  • Triple Alliance dominated neighboring altepetl (city-states) through conquest and tribute in food, textiles, and captives.
  • Chinampa agriculture (raised lake-bed gardens) supported a huge urban population.
  • Religion (Huitzilopochtli and other deities) legitimized rule and large-scale ritual, including human sacrifice.
  • A professional warrior elite and pochteca merchants tied the empire together.

The Inca (Tawantinsuyu) built the largest state in the pre-Columbian Americas along the Andes, using vertical ecology from coast to highlands to Amazon fringe.

  • Mit’a labor draft rotated communities into state work (roads, terraces, armies, storehouses).
  • Quipu (knotted cords) recorded census and tribute without alphabetic writing.
  • Road and way-station (qollqa/tambo) networks moved armies and food.
  • The Sapa Inca claimed descent from the sun; local huacas and conquered elites were incorporated.

Other American state systems included Mississippian chiefdoms (Cahokia’s mound complex had already peaked but the pattern of maize agriculture and ranked chiefdoms continued), Ancestral Pueblo communities of the Southwest (Chaco, Mesa Verde), and Maya city-states in Mesoamerica that continued, though Classic Maya political collapse had occurred earlier.

Across the Americas, states used religion, intensive agriculture (maize, potatoes), and tribute/labor systems rather than Old World wheeled transport or iron metallurgy as the backbone of power.
