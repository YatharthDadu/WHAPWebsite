Environmental Consequences of Connectivity

Learning objectives

  • Explain the environmental effects of the various networks of exchange in Afro-Eurasia from c. 1200 to c. 1450.
Progress 0% · Score 0

Travelers and merchants carried crops: bananas in Africa, new rice strains in East Asia, citrus in the Mediterranean. Agricultural diffusion supported population growth in some regions.

The most dramatic environmental-demographic shock was epidemic disease — especially bubonic plague moving along Mongol-era routes into the Middle East, North Africa, and Europe (Black Death). Urban crowding and trade made outbreaks worse. Labor shortages then reshaped wages and serfdom in parts of Europe and disrupted production across Eurasia.
