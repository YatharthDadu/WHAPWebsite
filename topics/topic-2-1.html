Topic 2.1

The Silk Roads

Learning objectives

  • Explain the causes and effects of growth of networks of exchange after 1200.
Progress 0% · Score 0

Improved commercial practices — bills of exchange, banking houses, paper money — and the security provided at times by large states (especially the Mongol Empire) expanded Silk Road trade. Luxury goods (silk, porcelain, spices, horses, precious metals) moved farther and more often.

New trading cities

  • Samarkand and Kashgar as oasis hubs.
  • Caravanserai (roadside inns) reduced risk for caravans.
  • Credit and money systems let merchants trade without carrying only coin.

Demand for luxury goods increased artisan production in China, Persia, and India. Cities along the routes flourished. Religions (Buddhism, Islam, Christianity) and technologies (papermaking, gunpowder) traveled with merchants.

The same networks later carried the bubonic plague. Silk Roads were not a single highway but a web of overland routes linking China to the Mediterranean via Central Asia.
