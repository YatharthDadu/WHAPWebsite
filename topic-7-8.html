Topic 7.8

Mass Atrocities After 1900

Learning objectives

  • Explain the various causes and consequences of mass atrocities in the period from 1900 to the present.
Progress 0% · Score 0

The Holocaust murdered about six million Jews and millions of others (Roma, disabled people, Poles, Soviet POWs, political opponents) through ghettos, shooting squads, and death camps. Other mass atrocities: Armenian genocide in the late Ottoman Empire; Ukrainian famine (Holodomor) under Stalin; Japanese atrocities in China (Nanjing); later Cambodian genocide, Rwandan genocide, and ethnic cleansing in the former Yugoslavia — the CED places mass atrocities across the century.

Causes include extreme nationalism, racist ideology, wartime opportunity, and state capacity. Consequences include refugee flows, human-rights law (UN Genocide Convention, Universal Declaration), and contested memory.
