c. 1200 to c. 1450

Excerpt from an edict by Song Dynasty Emperor Taizu:

"We must ensure that the officials who serve the empire are chosen not for their lineage, but for their mastery of the classical texts. Let the examination system be expanded, so that talent from all provinces may be discovered."

Source: Decree on the Civil Service Examinations, Song Dynasty, c. 960 CE (Adapted)

1. The policy described in the excerpt most directly reflects which of the following?

Explanation: The emphasis on mastery of classical texts and the examination system is a hallmark of Confucianism, which the Song dynasty used to maintain control and staff its bureaucracy.

2. The expansion of the examination system as mentioned most likely resulted in which of the following social changes?

Explanation: The expansion of the civil service exams allowed individuals to achieve status through education, contributing to the growth of the scholar-gentry.

3. Which of the following best describes the political effect of the policy in the excerpt?

Explanation: The civil service exam system helped the Song dynasty centralize power by creating a loyal bureaucracy dependent on the state rather than aristocratic lineage.

4. A historian would most likely use this excerpt to support which of the following arguments?

Explanation: The continued and expanded use of the examination system based on Confucian texts demonstrates how Chinese dynasties used traditional ideologies for state-building.

Excerpt from a traveler's account of Baghdad:

"The city is a marvel of scholarship and wealth. In the House of Wisdom, scholars from all over the world, regardless of their faith, gather to translate texts from Greek, Persian, and Indian languages into Arabic. Medicine, mathematics, and astronomy flourish under the patronage of the caliphs."

Source: Adapted from various traveler accounts of Abbasid Baghdad, c. 10th-12th centuries.

5. The developments described in the excerpt best illustrate which of the following?

Explanation: The House of Wisdom and the translation of various texts highlight how the Islamic world preserved and built upon knowledge from multiple cultures.

6. Which of the following political shifts later disrupted the intellectual flourishing described in the excerpt?

Explanation: The Mongol sack of Baghdad in 1258 destroyed the House of Wisdom and brought an end to the Abbasid Caliphate, disrupting this intellectual center.

7. The cross-cultural interactions described in the excerpt were most directly facilitated by which of the following?

Explanation: The Islamic world's location at the crossroads of Afro-Eurasia allowed it to act as a hub for both trade and intellectual exchange.

8. The gathering of scholars "regardless of their faith" reflects which of the following Islamic administrative practices?

Explanation: Islamic states often tolerated non-Muslims (like Christians and Jews) through the payment of the jizya tax, allowing them to contribute to society, including intellectual pursuits.

Excerpt from an inscription at a Khmer temple:

"The great king, a manifestation of Shiva on earth, constructed this vast reservoir and the temple complex to ensure the prosperity of the rice harvest and to demonstrate his divine authority over the realm."

Source: Fictionalized composite of inscriptions from the Khmer Empire (Angkor), c. 12th century.

9. The inscription best illustrates which of the following methods of state-building?

Explanation: The king claiming to be a manifestation of a Hindu deity (Shiva) shows how rulers used religious concepts to justify their authority.

10. The reference to constructing a "vast reservoir" highlights which of the following economic priorities of the Khmer Empire?

Explanation: Southeast Asian empires like the Khmer relied on complex irrigation and water management systems to support intense rice agriculture.

11. The presence of Hindu deities in a Southeast Asian kingdom is best explained by which of the following?

Explanation: Hinduism and Buddhism spread to Southeast Asia primarily through merchant activity and cultural exchange along Indian Ocean trade networks.

12. Which of the following later religious changes occurred in many parts of Southeast Asia, altering the cultural landscape described in the excerpt?

Explanation: In later centuries, Islam spread widely in Southeast Asia, particularly in maritime regions like Indonesia and Malaysia, shifting the religious landscape.

Excerpt from a Spanish chronicler describing the Inca Empire:

"They kept track of their population, tribute owed, and provisions stored in their granaries using a complex system of knotted strings. Without any written alphabet, these string experts could recount the history and resources of the empire with astonishing accuracy."

Source: Adapted from early colonial descriptions of the Inca quipu, c. 16th century.

13. The system of "knotted strings" described in the excerpt is known as:

Explanation: The quipu was a system of knotted cords used by the Inca to record numerical data and keep records.

14. The ability to track "tribute owed and provisions stored" best supports which of the following conclusions about the Inca state?

Explanation: The use of the quipu to track tribute and resources demonstrates the Inca's centralized bureaucratic control over their vast empire.

15. The system of tribute in the Inca Empire was most closely tied to which of the following labor systems?

Explanation: The Inca required a labor tax called the mit'a, where subjects had to provide labor for state projects like road building and agriculture.

16. Which of the following was a major geographic challenge that the administrative system described in the excerpt helped the Inca overcome?

Explanation: The Inca empire spanned the Andes; maintaining control over such mountainous terrain required excellent organization, communication, and record-keeping.

Excerpt from the Epic of Sundiata (oral tradition recorded later):

"Sundiata, the Lion King, gathered the clans of the Malinke. Through his great magic and military skill, he defeated the sorcerer king of Sosso. He then established the great empire of Mali, taking control of the gold and salt trade that crossed the great desert."

Source: Adapted from traditional accounts of the founding of Mali, c. 13th century.

17. The reference to taking control of the "gold and salt trade" indicates the economic foundation of the Mali Empire was based on:

Explanation: The Mali Empire grew wealthy by controlling the trans-Saharan trade routes, specifically the trade in gold from West Africa and salt from the Sahara.

18. The use of an oral epic to record the history of Sundiata reflects which of the following cultural practices in West Africa?

Explanation: Griots were specialized storytellers and musicians in West Africa who served as keepers of oral history and advisors to kings.

19. Which of the following later rulers of Mali is most famous for demonstrating the wealth mentioned in the excerpt to the broader Islamic world?

Explanation: Mansa Musa's famous hajj to Mecca showcased the immense wealth of Mali, particularly its gold, to the Islamic world.

20. The integration of "magic" and state-building in the excerpt suggests which of the following about religious practices in early Mali?

Explanation: Despite the growing influence of Islam, West African rulers often continued to draw legitimacy from traditional animist beliefs and claims of magical power.

Excerpt from the Magna Carta, 1215:

"No free man shall be seized or imprisoned, or stripped of his rights or possessions... except by the lawful judgment of his equals or by the law of the land. To no one will we sell, to no one deny or delay right or justice."

Source: The Magna Carta, signed by King John of England, 1215.

21. The Magna Carta was primarily a reaction against which of the following political conditions?

Explanation: The Magna Carta was forced upon King John by nobles to limit his power and protect their own rights, establishing the principle that the king was not above the law.

22. The excerpt best illustrates which of the following broader trends in medieval Europe?

Explanation: The Magna Carta is a foundational document for the concept of limited government and legal rights, contrasting with absolute monarchies.

23. Despite the language of the excerpt, which of the following groups was largely excluded from the protections of "free men" in 13th-century England?

Explanation: The vast majority of the population were serfs, who were tied to the land and not considered "free men" with the rights described in the Magna Carta.

24. The political structure of Europe during the period when this document was created is best described as:

Explanation: Medieval Europe was characterized by feudalism, a decentralized system based on land ownership, military service, and reciprocal obligations.

Excerpt from an account by a merchant traveling from Venice to China:

"From the city of Samarkand, you travel through vast deserts and high mountains for many days. Along the way, there are caravanserais where merchants can rest their camels and trade their wares safely. The safety of the route has increased recently, allowing more silk and porcelain to flow westward."

Source: Composite account based on the travels of Marco Polo and other Silk Road merchants, c. late 13th century.

25. The "recent increase in safety" mentioned in the excerpt is most directly attributable to which of the following?

Explanation: The Mongol Empire's vast control over Eurasia created a period of relative peace and stability (Pax Mongolica), which revitalized and protected the Silk Roads.

26. The mention of "caravanserais" highlights which of the following aspects of overland trade?

Explanation: Caravanserais were roadside inns built to support and protect the flow of commerce, demonstrating investment in trade infrastructure.

27. Which of the following financial innovations was most likely used by the merchants traveling these routes to facilitate trade?

Explanation: To avoid carrying heavy coins over long distances, Chinese innovations like paper money and letters of credit ("flying cash") became widely used on the Silk Roads.

28. Along with luxury goods like silk and porcelain, which of the following had the most significant demographic impact as it spread along the Silk Roads during this period?

Explanation: The same interconnectedness that facilitated trade also allowed the rapid spread of the Black Death, which devastated populations across Eurasia.

Excerpt from the Secret History of the Mongols:

"Chinggis [Genghis] Khan gave the following order: '...Those who submit to us shall be spared. But those who resist, their cities will be leveled to the ground, so that not a single brick remains, and their people shall be put to the sword as a warning to others.'"

Source: Adapted from The Secret History of the Mongols, c. 13th century.

29. The policy described in the excerpt was primarily used by the Mongols to:

Explanation: The Mongols deliberately used horrific violence against resisting cities as psychological warfare to convince other cities to surrender without a fight.

30. Which of the following was a direct result of the Mongol conquests described in the excerpt?

Explanation: Mongol conquests led to the fall of major political entities, including the Abbasid Caliphate and the Kievan Rus, reshaping the political map.

31. Despite the brutality of their conquests, how did Mongol rule eventually impact the cultures they conquered?

Explanation: During the Pax Mongolica, the Mongols actively moved skilled craftsmen and scholars across their empire, facilitating a massive transfer of knowledge and technology.

32. The Mongol administrative system in China (the Yuan Dynasty) is best characterized by which of the following?

Explanation: To maintain control and distrusting the native Han elite, the Yuan dynasty primarily employed Mongols and foreigners in high administrative roles.

Excerpt from an Arab navigator's manual:

"To sail to Calicut from the Arabian coast, one must wait for the summer months when the winds blow steadily from the southwest. Do not attempt the voyage in winter, for the winds will blow you back to the shores of Africa. By understanding these seasonal shifts, the journey is both swift and profitable."

Source: Composite of navigational texts used by Indian Ocean merchants, c. 14th century.

33. The "seasonal shifts" in wind patterns described in the excerpt are known as:

Explanation: The monsoon winds of the Indian Ocean predictably change direction with the seasons, which merchants used to plan their voyages.

34. The reliance on the wind patterns described in the excerpt most directly led to which of the following social phenomena in port cities?

Explanation: Because merchants had to wait months for the winds to change, they often settled temporarily or permanently in port cities, creating diasporic communities.

35. Which of the following technological innovations significantly aided the voyages described in the excerpt?

Explanation: Technologies like the astrolabe and the lateen sail were crucial for the expansion of Indian Ocean trade.

36. The trade networks mentioned in the excerpt primarily facilitated the exchange of which types of goods?

Explanation: Unlike overland routes, ships in the Indian Ocean could carry heavy bulk goods as well as luxury items.

Excerpt from description of visual stimulus: An illustration from a medieval European map (like the Catalan Atlas of 1375) showing a King in West Africa holding a large nugget of gold, with a camel merchant approaching him from the north.

Source: Description based on the Catalan Atlas, 1375.

37. The king depicted in the visual stimulus is most likely representing which historical figure?

Explanation: The Catalan Atlas famously depicts Mansa Musa holding a gold nugget, reflecting Europe's awareness of West African wealth.

38. The presence of the camel in the illustration highlights the importance of which of the following?

Explanation: The introduction of the camel saddle enabled camels to carry heavy loads across the arid Sahara, making the trans-Saharan trade possible.

39. Which of the following cultural changes was most directly facilitated by the trade network depicted in the image?

Explanation: Trans-Saharan trade brought Arab and Berber merchants to West Africa, leading to the gradual conversion of many West African rulers and elites to Islam.

40. The trade depicted in the map connected West Africa primarily with which of the following regions?

Explanation: The trans-Saharan trade routes connected the gold and salt economies of West Africa with the broader economies of North Africa, the Mediterranean, and the Middle East.

Excerpt from the writings of Ibn Battuta describing a visit to Mali:

"They are strict in observing the hours of prayer, studying books of law, and memorizing the Quran. Yet, their women show no bashfulness before men and do not veil themselves. This I found quite surprising, given their devotion to the faith."

Source: Travels of Ibn Battuta, c. 1350s.

41. The excerpt best illustrates which of the following processes associated with cultural connectivity?

Explanation: Ibn Battuta's surprise at the gender norms in Mali shows how Islam was adapted to fit local West African cultural practices.

42. Ibn Battuta’s ability to travel across Africa, the Middle East, and Asia was most directly facilitated by:

Explanation: The shared religion, language, and legal structures of Dar al-Islam allowed scholars and merchants like Ibn Battuta to travel extensively with relative ease.

43. The observations made in the excerpt suggest that in 14th-century Mali, gender roles were:

Explanation: The lack of veiling and free association between men and women indicate that gender roles in Mali were less restrictive than those Ibn Battuta was accustomed to in the Middle East.

44. A historian studying cross-cultural exchanges would use this passage to argue that:

Explanation: The passage shows that while Mali adopted Islam, they adapted it to their existing cultural norms rather than fully assimilating Middle Eastern practices.

Excerpt from a treatise by a medieval physician in Europe:

"In the year of our Lord 1348, a great pestilence struck our city. It originated in the East and moved along the trade routes, carried by rats and fleas. Those afflicted developed dark swellings and died within days. The streets were filled with the dead, and the fields lay unharvested."

Source: Fictionalized composite of contemporary accounts of the Black Death in Europe, c. mid-14th century.

45. The "great pestilence" described in the excerpt is best identified as:

Explanation: The description of dark swellings (buboes), rapid death, and origins in the East points directly to the Bubonic Plague.

46. The spread of the disease "along the trade routes" most directly implicates which of the following as a primary vector for the pandemic?

Explanation: The peace and increased trade fostered by the Mongol Empire inadvertently facilitated the rapid spread of the plague across Eurasia.

47. Which of the following was a major long-term economic consequence of the events described in the excerpt in Western Europe?

Explanation: The massive death toll caused a labor shortage, which allowed surviving peasants to demand better wages and lower rents, contributing to the decline of serfdom.

48. The reference to "fields lay unharvested" indicates that the pandemic had a significant impact on:

Explanation: The death of so many agricultural workers disrupted farming, leading to food shortages.

Excerpt from a historical analysis of the Indian Ocean network:

"Unlike the empires of land, no single state dominated the waves of the Indian Ocean during this era. Instead, a complex web of city-states, from Kilwa to Malacca, thrived on the flow of goods. They welcomed merchants of all faiths, building their wealth on commerce rather than conquest."

Source: Modern historical synthesis of Indian Ocean trade, 1200-1450.

49. The political structure of the Indian Ocean trade network described in the excerpt is best characterized as:

Explanation: The Indian Ocean trade was characterized by independent city-states rather than a single unifying empire.

50. The city of Malacca, mentioned in the excerpt, derived its wealth primarily from:

Explanation: Malacca controlled a crucial maritime choke point and grew incredibly wealthy by regulating and taxing the trade that flowed through its strait.

51. The cosmopolitan nature of these city-states ("welcomed merchants of all faiths") resulted in which of the following architectural developments?

Explanation: The diverse merchant populations led to a blending of cultures and the construction of various religious structures in these port cities.

52. How did the economic practices described in the excerpt differ from the state-building methods of the Mongol Empire during the same period?

Explanation: The Indian Ocean city-states built wealth through peaceful maritime trade, whereas the Mongol Empire built its wealth and power primarily through violent overland conquest and taxation.
