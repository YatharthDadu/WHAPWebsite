Topic 3.2

Empires: Administration

Learning objectives

  • Explain how rulers used a variety of methods to legitimize and consolidate their power in land-based empires from 1450 to 1750.
Progress 0% · Score 0

Rulers recruited bureaucrats and professional soldiers, standardized taxes, and used monumental architecture and court ritual.

  • Ottoman devshirme and janissaries — Christian boys levied, converted, and trained as elite infantry/administrators.
  • Ottoman tax farming; Mughal zamindar tax collection.
  • Qing examination system continued Confucian recruitment.
  • Russian boyars constrained as tsars centralized (Ivan IV, later Peter I).
  • Tokugawa Japan: bakufu, sankin-kotai (alternate attendance) to control daimyo — a land-based state even if not always labeled a ‘gunpowder empire’ in every textbook list.
  • Ottoman mosques (Süleymaniye); Mughal Taj Mahal and palaces.
  • Qing imperial portraits and the Forbidden City as a continuing stage.
  • Versailles as Louis XIV’s theater of absolutism in France.
  • Safavid Isfahan’s plazas and Shi’a shrines.
  • Inca and Mexica examples are earlier; in this period Spanish colonial states overlay those spaces.

Legitimacy mixed religion, genealogy, and spectacular building. Recruitment mixed merit, slavery, nobility, and conquest elites.
