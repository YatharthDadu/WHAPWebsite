Topic 1.7

Comparison in the Period from c. 1200 to c. 1450

Learning objectives

  • Explain the similarities and differences in the processes of state formation from c. 1200 to c. 1450.
Progress 0% · Score 0

Across Afro-Eurasia and the Americas, states used religion to legitimize rule, extracted surplus through tribute, tax, or labor, and built monumental architecture. Many expanded using military innovation (from cavalry and forts to early gunpowder in parts of Eurasia).

Common patterns

  • Religious claims: Mandate-like Confucian order, Islamic caliph/sultan, Christian kingship, Mexica gods, Inca sun descent.
  • Elite intermediaries: scholar-gentry, mamluks, samurai, nobles, tribute lords.
  • Trade revenues: Song commerce, Mali gold, Swahili ports, Srivijaya straits.
  • China: large, exam-based bureaucracy and commercial economy.
  • Dar al-Islam: political fragmentation after the Abbasids but shared legal-religious culture.
  • South/Southeast Asia: Hindu-Buddhist and Islamic states side by side; maritime vs. agrarian bases.
  • Americas: no Eastern Hemisphere livestock package or iron; sophisticated labor/tribute empires anyway.
  • Africa: trade-based Sudanic empires and coastal city-states; Ethiopia’s Christian continuity.
  • Europe: feudal fragmentation and a trans-regional Church.
