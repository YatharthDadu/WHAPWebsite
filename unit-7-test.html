Global Conflict

c. 1900 to present

Excerpt from the Constitution of the Russian Socialist Federated Soviet Republic (1918):

"The fundamental aim of the Constitution... is to abolish the exploitation of men by men, to entirely do away with the division of the people into classes, to suppress the exploiters, to establish a socialist organization of society, and to secure the victory of socialism in all lands."

Source: Russian Soviet Constitution, 1918.

1. The passage most directly reflects which major political shift of the early 20th century?

Explanation: The 1918 constitution explicitly outlines the goals of the new Bolshevik government after overthrowing the Russian Empire.

2. The stated goal to "secure the victory of socialism in all lands" contributed most directly to which later historical development?

Explanation: The explicit goal of spreading communism globally created long-term tensions with capitalist democracies, culminating in the Cold War.

Excerpt from an Austro-Hungarian government memorandum (July 1914):

"The assassination at Sarajevo clears away the false halo which surrounded the pan-Serbian movement... The monarchy must, therefore, tear the web which Serbia has woven around her... otherwise the monarchy will be faced with the disintegration of its own territories."

Source: Memorandum following the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand, 1914.

1. Based on the passage, the primary cause of conflict between Austria-Hungary and Serbia was driven by

Explanation: The "pan-Serbian movement" threatened to encourage nationalist uprisings and secession among Slavic groups within Austria-Hungary.

2. The event referenced in the passage ("The assassination at Sarajevo") acted as a catalyst for a global war primarily because of

Explanation: The localized conflict between Austria-Hungary and Serbia quickly escalated into a world war because treaties obligated other nations (like Russia, Germany, and France) to intervene.

Visual Description: A British propaganda poster from 1915 showing a determined woman pointing at the viewer with the text: "Women of Britain Say—'GO!'" Encouraging men to enlist in the military.

Source: Parliamentary Recruiting Committee, 1915.

1. The use of propaganda posters like the one described was indicative of which aspect of World War I?

Explanation: Total war required governments to use propaganda to mobilize all societal resources, including civilians and public opinion, to support the war effort.

2. The depiction of women encouraging men to enlist highlights how the war

Explanation: Women not only participated in recruitment but also entered the workforce in unprecedented numbers to replace men serving in the military.

Excerpt from Franklin D. Roosevelt's First Inaugural Address (1933):

"Our greatest primary task is to put people to work... It can be accomplished in part by direct recruiting by the Government itself, treating the task as we would treat the emergency of a war... hand in hand with this we must frankly recognize the overbalance of population in our industrial centers and... engage on a national scale in a redistribution."

Source: Franklin D. Roosevelt, 1933.

1. The passage outlines a response to which global economic crisis?

Explanation: FDR's speech addresses the massive unemployment and economic devastation caused by the Great Depression.

2. Roosevelt's proposed solution, which included "direct recruiting by the Government itself," represents a shift toward

Explanation: The New Deal marked a significant departure from laissez-faire policies, utilizing government spending and programs to stimulate the economy.

Excerpt from the Treaty of Versailles (1919), Article 231 (The "War Guilt Clause"):

"The Allied and Associated Governments affirm and Germany accepts the responsibility of Germany and her allies for causing all the loss and damage to which the Allied and Associated Governments and their nationals have been subjected as a consequence of the war imposed upon them by the aggression of Germany and her allies."

Source: Treaty of Versailles, 1919.

1. The inclusion of this clause in the Treaty of Versailles most directly contributed to

Explanation: The punitive nature of the treaty, particularly the acceptance of total blame and massive reparations, crippled Germany's economy and fostered a sense of humiliation exploited by the Nazi party.

2. The approach taken by the victorious powers in this treaty contrasts most sharply with

Explanation: Wilson had advocated for a more lenient and idealistic peace settlement, which was largely rejected by Britain and France in favor of punishing Germany.

Excerpt from Adolf Hitler's Mein Kampf (1925):

"We National Socialists must hold unflinchingly to our aim in foreign policy, namely, to secure for the German people the land and soil to which they are entitled on this earth... If we speak of soil in Europe today, we can primarily have in mind only Russia and her vassal border states."

Source: Adolf Hitler, Mein Kampf, 1925.

1. Based on the passage, a primary ideological cause of German expansionism leading to World War II was the pursuit of

Explanation: Hitler explicitly states the goal of acquiring territory ("land and soil") in Eastern Europe and Russia to support the German population.

2. The aggressive foreign policy advocated in this passage was initially met by Western powers with a policy of

Explanation: Britain and France initially responded to German expansion (e.g., the annexation of Austria and the Sudetenland) with appeasement to prevent war.

Excerpt from Winston Churchill's "Blood, Toil, Tears, and Sweat" speech (1940):

"You ask, what is our aim? I can answer in one word: It is victory, victory at all costs, victory in spite of all terror, victory, however long and hard the road may be; for without victory, there is no survival."

Source: Winston Churchill, House of Commons, 1940.

1. The tone and content of Churchill's speech reflect the reality that World War II was

Explanation: Churchill emphasizes that the war against Nazi Germany is an existential fight ("no survival") that demands absolute commitment ("victory at all costs").

2. A major technological development that significantly impacted the conduct and ultimate conclusion of the war was

Explanation: The development and use of atomic weapons by the United States brought a sudden and devastating end to the war in the Pacific.

Excerpt from the Nuremberg Laws (1935):

"1. Marriages between Jews and citizens of German or kindred blood are forbidden... 2. Extramarital intercourse between Jews and citizens of German or kindred blood is forbidden... A citizen of the Reich is only that subject... who is of German or kindred blood."

Source: The Nuremberg Race Laws, 1935.

1. The Nuremberg Laws were a critical step in the process that ultimately led to

Explanation: By legally defining Jews as a separate, inferior race and stripping them of citizenship, the Nazis laid the groundwork for the systematic extermination that followed.

2. Which of the following best describes the broader historical context of mass atrocities in the 20th century, such as those in Germany, Armenia, and Rwanda?

Explanation: Genocide and mass atrocities in the 20th century frequently leveraged the chaos of war and radical nationalist or racial ideologies to target specific ethnic or religious groups.

Excerpt from the United Nations Charter (1945):

"WE THE PEOPLES OF THE UNITED NATIONS DETERMINED to save succeeding generations from the scourge of war, which twice in our lifetime has brought untold sorrow to mankind, and to reaffirm faith in fundamental human rights... HAVE RESOLVED TO COMBINE OUR EFFORTS TO ACCOMPLISH THESE AIMS."

Source: Preamble to the Charter of the United Nations, 1945.

1. The creation of the United Nations, as described in the preamble, was primarily caused by the

Explanation: The UN was created explicitly to succeed where the League of Nations had failed: maintaining international peace and preventing another global war.

2. The emphasis on "fundamental human rights" in the passage reflects a response to which specific aspect of global conflict in the first half of the 20th century?

Explanation: The shocking scale of human rights abuses during WWII prompted a global consensus to explicitly define and protect human rights in the postwar international order.
