Topic 1.3

Developments in South and Southeast Asia from c. 1200 to c. 1450

Learning objectives

  • Explain how the various belief systems and practices of South and Southeast Asia affected society over time.
  • Explain how and why various states of South and Southeast Asia developed and maintained power over time.
Progress 0% · Score 0

Hinduism, Islam, and Buddhism continued to shape South and Southeast Asian societies. Core practices mixed with local traditions, producing both continuity and syncretism.

Illustrative examples

  • Bhakti movement — Hindu devotion emphasizing personal love of a deity; more accessible across caste lines.
  • Sufism — mystical Islam that helped conversion and cultural blending in South Asia.
  • Buddhist monasticism — especially important in Sri Lanka (Sinhala dynasties) and mainland Southeast Asia.

State formation showed continuity, innovation, and diversity. In the north, the Delhi Sultanate established Islamic rule over a Hindu majority. In the south, Hindu kingdoms persisted and regrouped.

  • Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526) — Turkic/Afghan dynasties; mosques and forts; jizya; cultural interaction.
  • Vijayanagara Empire (from 1336) — Hindu state in southern India that resisted northern sultanates and became a major temple and trade center.
  • Rajput kingdoms — Hindu warrior clans in northern/western India.
  • Sinhala dynasties — Buddhist states in Sri Lanka.

Southeast Asia sat on the hinge of Indian Ocean and China Sea trade. Indian religions (Hinduism and Buddhism) and later Islam arrived mainly with merchants, not mass settler conquest.

Illustrative examples

  • Khmer Empire — hydraulic rice state; Angkor Wat (Hindu, later more Buddhist).
  • Srivijaya — Buddhist maritime kingdom on Sumatra controlling the Strait of Malacca.
  • Majapahit — Hindu-Buddhist Java-based power after Srivijaya’s decline.
  • Sukhothai — early Thai kingdom.

Rulers used religious legitimacy, monumental temples, and control of trade chokepoints to maintain power. The region stayed politically diverse — no single empire ruled all of South and Southeast Asia.
