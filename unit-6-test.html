Unit 6 Exam

Consequences of Industrialization

c. 1750 to c. 1900

Progress 0% · Score 0

Excerpt from Josiah Strong, Our Country (1885):

"It seems to me that God, with infinite wisdom and skill, is training the Anglo-Saxon race for an hour sure to come in the world's future... Then this race of unequaled energy... will spread itself over the earth. If I read not amiss, this powerful race will move down upon Mexico, down upon Central and South America, out upon the islands of the sea, over upon Africa and beyond."

Source: Josiah Strong, Our Country: Its Possible Future and Its Present Crisis, 1885.

1. Based on the passage, the author's justification for imperialism is most heavily influenced by which of the following ideologies?

Explanation: Strong uses language of racial destiny and "unequaled energy" to justify the expansion and domination of the Anglo-Saxon race over others, a hallmark of Social Darwinist thought.

2. Which of the following best describes the broader historical context of the passage?

Explanation: The late 19th century saw a surge in imperial expansion as industrializing nations sought new territories, justified by ideologies like those expressed in the passage.

Excerpt from the General Act of the Berlin Conference (1885):

"In order to avoid the misunderstandings and disputes which might in future arise from new acts of occupation on the coast of the African Continent... Any power which henceforth takes possession of a tract of land on the coasts of the African Continent outside of its present possessions... shall acquire them on the condition of establishing an authority in the regions occupied, sufficient to protect existing rights."

Source: General Act of the Berlin Conference, 1885.

1. The primary purpose of the Berlin Conference, as reflected in the excerpt, was to

Explanation: The conference was convened to manage European competition for African territory and prevent conflict among the colonizing powers.

2. The requirement to establish "an authority in the regions occupied" (the principle of effective occupation) most directly led to

Explanation: European powers rapidly moved inland to establish administrative control and validate their territorial claims, leading to the rapid colonization of almost the entire continent.

Excerpt from a letter by Ndansi Kumalo, an Ndebele warrior, recalling the 1896 Matabele Rebellion against the British South Africa Company:

"We were treated like slaves. They came and were the masters of our land, and we were the servants... Then the rebellion broke out... We had no guns, only spears and shields, but we fought them. The white men had the Maxim guns, and they mowed us down like grass."

Source: Ndansi Kumalo, recounting the Matabele Rebellion, recorded in the 1930s.

1. Based on the passage, a primary cause of the Matabele Rebellion was

Explanation: Kumalo cites being treated like slaves and losing their land as the core grievances leading to the rebellion.

2. The outcome of the battle described by Kumalo illustrates which broader trend of the period c. 1750–1900?

Explanation: The mention of Maxim guns (early machine guns) mowing down warriors with spears highlights the technological gap that facilitated European imperialism.

Visual Description: A chart showing global guano (seabird excrement used as fertilizer) exports from Peru between 1840 and 1880, showing a massive increase from near zero to hundreds of thousands of tons annually, primarily exported to Britain and the United States.

Source: Based on historical trade data of the Peruvian guano boom.

1. The dramatic increase in guano exports shown in the chart was primarily driven by

Explanation: Industrialization led to population growth and a greater need for food, driving demand for powerful fertilizers like guano to increase agricultural yields.

2. The economic relationship depicted in the chart is characteristic of which broader pattern during this period?

Explanation: Peru's economy became heavily reliant on exporting a single raw material to industrialized nations, a common pattern in the global economic system of the time.

Excerpt from the Treaty of Nanjing (1842), ending the First Opium War:

"His Majesty the Emperor of China agrees that British Subjects, with their families and establishments, shall be allowed to reside, for the purpose of carrying on their Mercantile pursuits, without molestation or restraint at the Cities and Towns of Canton, Amoy, Foochow-fu, Ningpo, and Shanghai."

Source: Treaty of Nanjing, 1842.

1. The provisions of the Treaty of Nanjing most directly illustrate which concept?

Explanation: The treaty forced China to open specific ports to British trade on unequal terms, a key example of economic imperialism where a powerful nation exerts economic control over a weaker one.

2. A major long-term consequence of the treaty excerpted above was

Explanation: The Treaty of Nanjing was the first of many "unequal treaties" imposed by Western powers (and later Japan) that severely weakened the Qing Dynasty's control over its own territory and economy.

Excerpt from a letter by an Irish immigrant in the United States to his family in Ireland (1848):

"My Dear Father and Mother... The blight has taken all the potatoes again... Here in America, there is plenty of work for strong hands. I am earning more in a week than I could in a month at home. Tell my brothers they must save their passage money and come as soon as they can. Do not stay to starve."

Source: Fictionalized composite based on typical Irish immigrant letters from the 1840s.

1. Based on the passage, the primary push factor for this migration was

Explanation: The letter references the "blight" and the threat of starving, pointing directly to the Irish Potato Famine as the main cause for leaving.

2. The mention of "plenty of work" in America highlights which broader trend of 19th-century migration?

Explanation: The growing industries and infrastructure projects in places like the United States created a high demand for labor, attracting millions of migrants seeking economic opportunity.

Excerpt from the Chinese Exclusion Act, passed by the US Congress (1882):

"Whereas in the opinion of the Government of the United States the coming of Chinese laborers to this country endangers the good order of certain localities within the territory thereof: Therefore, Be it enacted... That from and after the expiration of ninety days next after the passage of this act... the coming of Chinese laborers to the United States be, and the same is hereby, suspended."

Source: The Chinese Exclusion Act, 1882.

1. The passage most directly reflects which response to global migration in the late 19th century?

Explanation: The Chinese Exclusion Act was a direct result of nativist sentiment and racial prejudice, representing the first significant law restricting immigration into the US on the basis of race or nationality.

2. One consequence of the attitudes reflected in this act was

Explanation: Facing discrimination and exclusion, immigrant groups often formed ethnic enclaves (like Chinatowns) to support each other socially and economically.

Excerpt from J.A. Hobson, Imperialism: A Study (1902):

"Imperialism is the endeavor of the great controllers of industry to broaden the channel for the flow of their surplus wealth by seeking foreign markets and foreign investments to take off the goods and capital they cannot sell or use at home."

Source: J.A. Hobson, Imperialism: A Study, 1902.

1. Based on the passage, Hobson identifies which of the following as the primary cause of imperialism?

Explanation: Hobson explicitly links imperialism to the need for industrial nations to find outlets for their "surplus wealth," goods, and capital.

2. Hobson's view, as expressed in the excerpt, best supports which broader historical argument about the period c. 1750–1900?

Explanation: By arguing that industrialization created surpluses that drove overseas expansion, Hobson supports the argument that the Industrial Revolution was the key catalyst for the "New Imperialism."
