Industrialization created both the means (steam, guns, medicine such as quinine) and the motives (materials, markets, nationalism) for a new wave of empire. Colonized peoples supplied commodities and labor, including long-distance migrants. Ideologies of race justified hierarchy. Resistance never disappeared. By 1900 much of Africa and large parts of Asia were under formal or informal imperial control — a principal cause of 20th-century global conflict and decolonization.
